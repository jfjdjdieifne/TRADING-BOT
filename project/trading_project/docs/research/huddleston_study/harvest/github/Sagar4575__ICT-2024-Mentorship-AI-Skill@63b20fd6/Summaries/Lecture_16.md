# ICT 2024 Mentorship - Lecture 16 Summary
**Date:** August 24, 2024 (Review and Chart Annotation)

## 1. Charting, Journaling, and Pattern Recognition
*   **The Routine:** Developing the skill to read algorithmic price delivery requires daily charting and journaling. You must take screenshots of your charts every session, annotating the PD Arrays (gaps, order blocks, liquidity pools) before, during, and after they are hit.
*   **Pattern Recognition:** Even if you do not take a trade, logging the setups builds subconscious pattern recognition. "Repetition provides the recognition." Over time, the setups will become obvious before they execute.

## 2. Advanced PD Arrays: The Event Horizon
*   **Definition ("Gray Pool"):** The Event Horizon is a highly advanced, hidden PD array that exists between gaps. It acts like a "black hole" drawing price aggressively into it.
*   **How to Calculate:** Find two opening gaps that are in close proximity to each other. This can be a New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) and a New Week Opening Gap (NWOG), two NDOGs, or two NWOGs. Draw a Fibonacci level from the boundary of one gap to the boundary of the other. The **midpoint** of that space is the Event Horizon.
*   **Lookback Period:** You can use gaps up to **60 days old** to calculate an Event Horizon. Gaps older than 60 days (or 3 months maximum) generally lose their significance for this specific measurement.
*   **Ideal Setup:** An Event Horizon is most powerful when it perfectly aligns ("nests") with a pool of relative equal highs or relative equal lows.

## 3. Balanced Price Ranges and Propulsion Blocks
*   **Balanced Price Range (BPR):** A specific range where price has chopped back and forth, offering both buy-side and sell-side delivery efficiently. 
*   **BPR Mechanics:** Once price decisively breaks out of a balanced price range, it becomes a "brick wall." Price should not easily trade back through it. If the algorithm allows price to slice back through an established balanced price range, it strongly indicates manual intervention (e.g., major news events) and the technical setup is likely invalidated.
*   **Propulsion Block:** When price changes its state of delivery by crossing over an established order block, the very next subsequent order block that accelerates the move away from the original block is called a Propulsion Block. It propels price aggressively in the new direction.

## 4. Inversion Fair Value Gaps
*   **Flipping Polarity:** When a standard Fair Value Gap (FVG) is decisively traded through and violated by algorithmic displacement, it does not become useless. It flips its polarity.
*   **Usage:** A broken bullish FVG becomes bearish resistance, and a broken bearish FVG becomes bullish support. This is known as an Inversion Fair Value Gap. Watch how the bodies of the candles respect the boundaries of the gap upon a retest from the opposite side.
