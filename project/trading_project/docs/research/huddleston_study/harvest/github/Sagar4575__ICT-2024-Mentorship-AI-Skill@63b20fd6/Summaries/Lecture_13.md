# ICT 2024 Mentorship - Lecture 13 Summary
**Date:** August 20, 2024 (Begins 9:15 AM ET)

## 1. Important Gaps Defined
*   **New Day Opening Gap (NDOG):** The space or price difference between the previous session's settlement price (5:00 PM EST) and the current session's electronic opening price (6:00 PM EST). This gap can be carried forward up to five days as a highly active reference point.
*   **Opening Range Gap (9:30 AM Gap):** The gap between the previous session's settlement price (4:15 PM EST for indices) and the regular trading hours open at 9:30 AM EST. 
*   **Daily Volume Imbalance:** An inefficiency on the daily chart where two consecutive daily candles have no overlapping bodies, connecting only via their wicks. ICT breaks these large imbalances into quadrants (quarters) to use as high-timeframe targets.

## 2. Trading the 9:30 AM Opening Gap
*   **The "Half-Gap" Rule:** If the market gaps down significantly at 9:30 AM relative to the previous day's settlement, immediately draw a Fibonacci level to mark the 50% midpoint of that gap. 
*   **The Draw:** The market will almost universally gravitate back up to the midpoint (Consequent Encroachment) of that gap during the morning session. This is a very reliable, high-probability "bread and butter" trade that doesn't even require a longer-term directional bias.

## 3. Managing Trades and Taking Partials
*   **The Professional Mindset:** Taking partial profits is the hallmark of a professional trader. The social media concept of "all or nothing" (holding the full position until the final target is hit) is toxic and counterproductive for developing traders.
*   **When to Take Partials:** When you are long and in a buy program, *every short-term high that is broken* is an immediate candidate to take partial profits. 
*   **Stop Loss Management:** It is far better to take a partial profit and then move your stop loss to breakeven, than it is to prematurely trail your stop loss tightly to "lock in profits." Trailing a stop too tightly before taking partials usually results in getting knocked out of a winning trade during normal algorithmic retracements.

## 4. Caleb’s Core Trading Model
*   **The Framework:** The simplified trading model ICT is teaching his son revolves entirely around gaps and liquidity pools.
*   **The Setup:** 
    1. Identify a higher timeframe draw on liquidity, such as a New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) or New Week Opening Gap (NWOG).
    2. Identify Relative Equal Highs (buy-side liquidity) or Relative Equal Lows (sell-side liquidity) resting near that gap.
    3. Enter on a lower timeframe Fair Value Gap (FVG) or Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill (IOED) that aligns with the direction of the draw.
    4. Target the relative equal highs/lows and take partials aggressively.

## 5. Mohawks and Wicks
*   **Mohawks:** A "Mohawk" occurs when a wick pokes slightly outside the defined boundaries of a Fair Value Gap, but the body of the candle closes firmly inside or above the gap.
*   **Wicks Do the Damage:** Wicks are allowed to pierce gaps or support levels to trigger stops ("doing the damage"). However, the *bodies* of the candles tell the true story of algorithmic support. If the bodies respect the gap, the setup is valid.
*   **Consequent Encroachment of Wicks:** Wicks themselves are treated as gaps. The exact midpoint of a long wick (Consequent Encroachment) acts as highly precise support or resistance on sub-minute charts (like the 15-second chart).
