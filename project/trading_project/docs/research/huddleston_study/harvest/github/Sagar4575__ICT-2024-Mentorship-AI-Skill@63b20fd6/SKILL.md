### Determining Bias: "Smoothness" vs "Jaggedness" [Lecture 1]
- **Smoothness (Still Waters):** The market will draw towards areas of "smoothness" to disrupt orders (stop-losses). Smoothness is defined by **Relative Equal Highs** or **Relative Equal Lows**. 
- **Jaggedness (Shark Attacks):** Areas where price has already been disrupted, spiked through, or chopped up (like shark teeth marks). Do not expect price to draw into areas that have already been heavily cannibalized.

### High-Probability Relative Equal Highs / Lows [Lecture 1]
- **High-Probability Relative Equal Highs (Priming):** When you have two swing highs, and the one on the **left** is slightly higher than the one on the **right**. This creates a trap that makes retail think it's strong resistance. The algorithm will likely sweep above this.
- **High-Probability Relative Equal Lows (Failure Swing):** When you have two swing lows, and the one on the left is slightly lower than the one on the **right** (a higher low). 

*Bias Rule:* Look at price *prior* to the Pre-Market Windows. Whichever side has the smooth relative equal highs/lows is your bias. If both sides have them, wait for the first one to be disrupted, then target the other.

## Technical PD Arrays (Premium/Discount Arrays) Defined [Lecture 1]
- **Displacement:** When the market runs aggressively against a pre-session, pre-day trend, or price swing direction.
- **Bullish Breaker:** When the market drops and takes out relative equal lows, every up-close candle in that drop is part of the breaker. 
  - *Key Detail:* The most sensitive part of the breaker is the **last up-close candle** before the drop. When price returns to it, look for the *bodies* of the candles to respect it, even if wicks pierce it.
- **Fair Value Gap (FVG):** An inefficiency in price delivery.
  - **SIBI (Sell-side Imbalance, Buy-side Inefficiency):** A fair value gap with a down-close candle.
  - **BISI (Buy-side Imbalance, Sell-side Inefficiency):** A fair value gap with an up-close candle.
- **Volume Imbalance:** A slight separation or gap between the bodies/wicks of consecutive candles.
- **Inversion Fair Value Gap:** An old Fair Value Gap that price trades completely through, which then acts as support/resistance in the opposite direction.
- **Consequent Encroachment (CE):** The midpoint (50% level) of an inefficiency/gap or a price leg.
- **New Week Opening Gap (NWOG):** The gap between Friday's close and Sunday's 6:00 PM EST open. It acts as a massive draw on liquidity.

## Advanced PD Arrays & Inefficiencies [Lecture 2]
### Types of Fair Value Gaps (FVGs) [Lecture 2]

- **BISI (Buy-side Imbalance, Sell-side Inefficiency):** A fair value gap formed by an **up-close** candle.

### Volume Imbalances inside FVGs [Lecture 2]
- A **Volume Imbalance** is a gap between the *bodies* of consecutive candles, even if their wicks overlap.
- *Crucial Rule:* If a Fair Value Gap contains a Volume Imbalance, **ignore the wicks**. The true sensitivity of the gap lies strictly between the candle bodies. Wicks inside this area are just "coloring outside the lines."

### Inversion Fair Value Gaps [Lecture 2]
- An FVG that price trades completely through. Once broken, it reverses its role (e.g., an old BISI becomes resistance for lower prices). This is one of the highest precision levels in price action.

### The Most Sensitive FVG (Stop Hunt Anchor) [Lecture 2]
- The highest probability FVG is the **very first one formed just prior to a stop run**. If the market runs stops and reverses, look for the first gap that formed before the stops were taken.
- If this specific FVG does not form, fall back to using a standard **Breaker** (the last down-close candle before the run up that took stops).

## Gap Theory: NDOG and NWOG [Lecture 3]
The most magnetic draws on liquidity are time-based gaps that represent true imbalances (areas where no trading occurred). 

### New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) [Lecture 3]
- **Formation:** Formed by the difference between the 5:00 PM EST market close and the 6:00 PM EST market reopen (applies to Index markets like Nasdaq/S&P).
- **Life Cycle:** 5 trading days.
- **Application:** Keep a running log of the NDOGs from the past 5 trading days. They act as "black holes" or magnets pulling price toward them, especially if they remain untested.

### New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) [Lecture 3]
- **Formation:** Formed by the difference between Friday's 5:00 PM EST close and Sunday's 6:00 PM EST reopen.
- **Life Cycle:** 5 trading weeks.
- **Application:** Because it spans a larger timeframe, it exerts a stronger gravitational pull on price than an NDOG.

### Gap Quadrants and Consequent Encroachment (CE) [Lecture 3]
- **Consequent Encroachment:** The exact midpoint (50%) of a gap.
- Divide any gap into quadrants (25%, 50%, 75%).
- A highly sensitive FVG or Gap will hold price exactly at its Consequent Encroachment or one of its quadrants. 
- *Strength Signature:* If a candle approaches a gap but fails to even touch its Consequent Encroachment before reversing, it signals extreme underlying strength/heaviness in the opposing direction.

## Core Technical Concepts & Topics [Lecture 8]

### 1. The Change in the State of Delivery [Lecture 8]
- Represents the precise moment the algorithm switches its operating script from buy-side delivery to sell-side delivery (or vice versa).
- **Bearish Order Block Validation:** When price breaks down and closes below the opening price of the highest up-close candle(s) that formed a swing high, the state of delivery flips to sell-side. That exact opening price becomes the key reference level and the true order block.

### 2. Advanced Wick Analysis & Wicks as Targets [Lecture 8]
- Always measure wicks (swing highs/lows) precisely from the absolute tip to the candle body.
- The **Consequent Encroachment** (midpoint) of a wick is a critical algorithmic level. Treat a wick as if it were a gap.
- If a price retracement fails to reach the consequent encroachment of a wick and immediately reverses, it indicates significant algorithmic weakness in that direction.
- **Wick Projection:** When projecting a target below a major low that terminates in a wick, measure the range of the wick. Project 25% (0.25) of that range below the low to find the next algorithmic liquidity target.

### 3. Balanced Price Ranges Inside FVGs [Lecture 8]
- If a fair value gap forms, but the prior candle and the following candle's bodies overlap—leaving the gap completely void of wicks—this creates a **Balanced Price Range** inside the inefficiency.
- Price will often flush through this zone quickly to overlap the void, hit the opposing body, and tap out without continuing deeper.

### 4. Inversion Fair Value Gaps [Lecture 8]

*   **The Hunting Analogy:** When backtesting, you are a hunter looking *only* for the deer track. Ignore all other noise and variations. Focus strictly on mastering one footprint.


## 1. Trade Management & Taking Partials [Lecture 19]

### The Dynamic Trail Partial [Lecture 19]
When the market has broken heavily into your favor (e.g., dropping below a major daily low) and you want to hold for a massive runner without giving up guaranteed profit:
*   Wait for price to slice through the target liquidity pool and start accelerating.
*   Instead of placing a fixed limit order to take partials right below the old low (which often cuts you out of huge extensions), use a dynamic trail.
*   **The Technique:** Identify each successive down-close candle that creates an imbalance as price drops. Place a Buy Stop (to cover a partial) exactly one tick above the high of that candle. 
*   Continue trailing this Buy Stop down with every new one-minute candlestick high as it spools lower. You will get knocked out of your partial dynamically, often incredibly close to the absolute low of the move, while retaining the rest of your core position.

### Grading Price Swings ("Heaviness") [Lecture 19]
*   When price is targeting a massive draw on liquidity (like an old daily low), it should act "heavy."
*   **Signatures of Heaviness:** Lower highs on every candle, failure to close the upper half of bearish fair value gaps, and immediate rejection from premium arrays. It shouldn't dally; it should drop quickly.

## 2. Market Structure & Retail Traps [Lecture 19]

### The "Fake Bull Flag" [Lecture 19]
*   **Setup:** When the algorithm is fundamentally predisposed to go lower, it will often bounce and form relative equal highs on a retracement.
*   **The Trap:** Retail traders see this consolidation and slant it upward, drawing trendlines and calling it a "bull flag breakout."
*   **The Reality:** The algorithm is simply engineering a shallow pool of buy stops above those relative equal highs. It sweeps them quickly, spends virtually no time up there, and then displaces aggressively lower into the true draw on liquidity.

### The Breakaway Gap & The Institutional Order Entry Drill [Lecture 19]
*   **Breakaway Gap:** An inefficiency created right after a major shift in market structure or as price rips away from a consolidation. It signals extreme momentum. Ideally, a true Breakaway Gap remains unfilled.
*   **Institutional Order Entry Drill:** When price returns to a gap, but only pierces it by one or two ticks before immediately violently rejecting, leaving the maximum amount of the gap's range open. This is a hallmark of algorithmic precision and confirms the Breakaway Gap's validity.

### The 3-Day Lookback [Lecture 19]
*   The highest high and lowest low of the last **three trading days** (excluding Sundays) are paramount. The algorithm uses time as its primary reference point, and these levels hold massive clusters of institutional stop-loss orders. Price will constantly gravitate to them.

### The 2:50 PM Spooling Macro [Lecture 19]
*   At exactly 2:50 PM EST, the late afternoon algorithm kicks in. 
*   **The Fishing Analogy:** If the draw on liquidity is obvious (like a resting pool of stops just above/below price), the algorithm acts like a fishing reel spooling line. It will accelerate violently ("spooling") to hit that Terminus directly before the 4:00 PM close.


## 1. Trade Psychology & Eliminating Angst [Lecture 20]

### The Half-Size De-stressor [Lecture 20]
*   **The Dilemma:** Developing traders often enter a position, see the market stall or start to look "heavy," and immediately panic. They debate whether to close the full position, risking missing a move if they are right.
*   **The Solution:** The absolute instant you feel that anxiety rising and start questioning the technical validity of the setup, **cut the trade size in half**.
*   **The Logic:** You reduce the emotional and financial pressure. If it was a 1-contract trade, close it entirely and "tape read" the rest. This preserves capital and trains you to monitor price objectively without the distortion of realized drawdowns.

### Sitting Still (The Win-Win) [Lecture 20]
*   You never lose money sitting on the sidelines. If the framework requires forced 50/50 guesses (e.g., conflicting PD Arrays, tightly clustered old gaps), it is considered **High Resistance**. 
*   **The Spider Web Analogy:** When New Day Opening Gaps, New Week Opening Gaps, and old highs/lows are heavily layered over a small price range, they act like a spider web. Price (the fly) gets tangled and chops back and forth. You want to trade when the fly escapes the web and heads straight for the sugar (open liquidity).

## 2. Risk/Reward and Stop-Loss Management [Lecture 20]

### The 5-to-1 Gearing Model [Lecture 20]
*   The goal is to structure trades where the target yields five times the initial risk (e.g., risk 18 handles to make 90 handles).
*   With a 5:1 reward-to-risk ratio (gearing), you can afford to lose over 70% of your trades and still be net profitable. It removes the pressure of needing a high win rate.

### Stop Management & Commission Costs [Lecture 20]
*   When a trade begins moving in your favor, identify the distance to your **First Partial** (usually a logical short-term high/low or the midpoint of an inefficiency).
*   When price moves **halfway** to your first partial target, move your stop loss to cover entry **plus commission costs** (e.g., +0.5 to +1 handle). Do not just move it to "break even," as closing at break even still results in a net loss due to fees.

## 3. Advanced Concepts [Lecture 20]

### SMT Divergence (Smart Money Tool) [Lecture 20]
*   Compare correlated assets like NQ (NASDAQ) and ES (S&P 500).
*   If NQ makes a higher high, but ES fails to make a higher high at the exact same time, that divergence is an SMT. 
*   It does *not* necessarily mean an instant trend reversal, but it strongly indicates that bullish momentum is waning and the probability of a pullback or sideways chop is elevated.

### The Institutional Order Entry Drill (Revisited) [Lecture 20]
*   When a Breakaway Gap or an FVG is tapped by only a single tick (or minimal ticks) and immediately aggressively rejects, leaving almost the entire inefficiency open.
*   The stop loss goes one tick below the low of the *imbalance candle* (the middle candle of the 3-bar FVG sequence).


## 1. High-Resistance Market Environments [Lecture 21]

### Pre-Holiday Price Action [Lecture 21]
*   **The Scenario:** Fridays leading into long holiday weekends (e.g., Labor Day) are notoriously difficult. Large entities (deep pockets) are usually sidelined or simply managing existing positions, meaning the algorithmic engine lacks organic institutional order flow to pair against.
*   **The Symptoms:** Price action becomes "muddy," disorganized, and schizophrenic. It feels like an anxiety attack—chopping back and forth with no clear directive.
*   **The "Spider Web" Analogy:** When major PD Arrays (New Week Opening Gaps, New Day Opening Gaps, Old Highs/Lows) are clustered closely together, they act like a spider web. Price (the fly) gets tangled in them, causing heavy consolidation. 
*   **The Solution:** Do not force your edge. Sit on your hands. Overtrading in these conditions dulls your edge and damages your psychology.

### Immediate Rebalance [Lecture 21]
*   A signature of strong momentum. If a candle expands aggressively, and the very next candle immediately wicks back to exactly overlap the previous candle's body, touching the open/close of the candle prior to the expansion. 
*   If price is genuinely directional, it should "giddy-up" and violently reject from this immediate rebalance point.

## 2. Order Flow & Algorithmic Mechanics [Lecture 21]

### The Fallacy of Volume & Buying/Selling Pressure [Lecture 21]
*   ICT reiterates that price is not strictly driven by heavy buying or selling pressure. 
*   **The One-Contract Rule:** It only takes one single contract pairing a buyer and a seller to print a tick on the tape. 
*   The algorithm delivers price to areas of *inefficiency* (gaps) and *liquidity* (stops) simply because it is programmed to offer fair value and seek liquidity, not because a massive imbalance of buy orders forced the market up.

### Floor Traders vs. Electronic Trading [Lecture 21]
*   **The Floor Trader's Edge:** In the days of open outcry, pit traders had the advantage of feeling the "fevered pitch" of the crowd. They could sense momentum and target obvious levels like yesterday's high or low.
*   **The Electronic Transition:** When trading moved to screens, floor traders lost their sensory edge. Many struggle today because they rely on laggy indicators (VWAP, Volume Profile) to replace that lost "feel," instead of understanding algorithmic delivery.
