# ICT 2024 Mentorship - Lecture 2 Summary (August 6, 2024)

## Chart Setup and Organization
You only need a specific set of intraday timeframes. Do not use anything higher than a 15-minute chart.
- **Upper Left:** 15-Minute Chart
- **Lower Left:** 5-Minute Chart
- **Upper Right:** 1-Minute Chart
- **Lower Right (Optional):** 15-Second Chart
  - *Note on 15-Second Chart:* Used only for extreme precision. It is very forgiving and allows you to catch a move if you missed the ideal 1-minute entry. It should not replace your understanding of higher timeframes.

## Journaling and Psychology
- **Be Your Own Coach, Not a Drill Sergeant:** When reviewing your trades or missed opportunities, do not beat yourself up. You are coaching someone you love (yourself). Filter out toxic people, social media noise, and negative self-talk.
- **Do Not Anticipate, Wait for the Setup:** Never predict what the market will do before the chart prints the signature. Let the setup form first.
- **Novelty and Boredom:** Trading should be boring and monotonous. If you are seeking adrenaline or a rush, you are gambling. Be content with doing very little and taking small profits initially.

## The 7:00 AM Rule (Start of AM Session)
- **Ignore London (Pre-7:00 AM) Liquidity:** For beginners, do NOT look for relative equal highs or relative equal lows that formed *prior* to 7:00 AM New York Local Time.
- **The Hunt Begins at 7:00 AM:** 7:00 AM EST is your "punch-in time". You start looking for relative equal highs or relative equal lows to form *after* 7:00 AM.
- If the market is drawn to the London session highs/lows, it is for advanced traders (New York Session Market Reversal Profile). Keep it simple and focus only post-7:00 AM.

## Pre-Market Ranges and Time Windows
The algorithm operates on strict 30-minute cycles following the top of the hour:
1. **7:00 AM to 7:30 AM**
2. **8:00 AM to 8:30 AM**
3. **9:00 AM to 9:30 AM**
- *Expectation:* During the first 30 minutes of these hours, expect a **Judith Swing** (manipulation). The market will initially run in the *opposite* direction of the true draw on liquidity.

## Advanced PD Arrays & Inefficiencies
### Types of Fair Value Gaps (FVGs)
- **SIBI (Sell-side Imbalance, Buy-side Inefficiency):** A fair value gap formed by a **down-close** candle.
- **BISI (Buy-side Imbalance, Sell-side Inefficiency):** A fair value gap formed by an **up-close** candle.

### Volume Imbalances inside FVGs
- A **Volume Imbalance** is a gap between the *bodies* of consecutive candles, even if their wicks overlap.
- *Crucial Rule:* If a Fair Value Gap contains a Volume Imbalance, **ignore the wicks**. The true sensitivity of the gap lies strictly between the candle bodies. Wicks inside this area are just "coloring outside the lines."

### Inversion Fair Value Gaps
- An FVG that price trades completely through. Once broken, it reverses its role (e.g., an old BISI becomes resistance for lower prices). This is one of the highest precision levels in price action.

### The Most Sensitive FVG (Stop Hunt Anchor)
- The highest probability FVG is the **very first one formed just prior to a stop run**. If the market runs stops and reverses, look for the first gap that formed before the stops were taken.
- If this specific FVG does not form, fall back to using a standard **Breaker** (the last down-close candle before the run up that took stops).

## Projecting Targets using Fibonacci
To find potential expansion targets when the market breaks out, use Fibonacci extensions anchored to specific times:
- Measure from the High to the Lowest Low formed right at **7:00 AM, 8:00 AM, or 9:00 AM**.
- Use standard deviations of **1, 2, and 2.5** to gauge where price might stretch if it enters a protracted run. These are not exact targets, but areas of confluence to look for other PD arrays.
