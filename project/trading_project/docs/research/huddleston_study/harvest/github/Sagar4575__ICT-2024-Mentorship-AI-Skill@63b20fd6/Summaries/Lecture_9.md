## Core Technical Concepts & Topics

### 1. Election Year Tendencies
- During election years, the stock market typically has a built-in "buy model" or upward bias.
- The objective is often to artificially inflate the market to make the incumbent administration look favorable economically. 
- *Rule:* Because the market is pre-disposed to press higher, attempting to short every perceived top or "broken wing" setup will likely result in being chopped up.

### 2. Premium Pricing & Refusing to Chase Price
- When the market opens with a large gap up and immediately runs higher (creating a highly extended premium environment), do not chase the breakout.
- "Dumb money" buys momentum at the highs; professionals wait for a return to a discount.
- *Discipline:* Wait for a retracement back to structural support, such as the 9:30 AM opening price, an old gap, or a liquidity pool below an old low, before considering a long position. If it runs without you, accept it and wait for the next setup.

### 3. The 9:30 AM Opening Price (Fair Value)
- The opening price at 9:30 AM (New York local time) establishes the baseline for the session.
- Even in strong trending environments, after an initial morning protraction (run away from the open), price frequently returns to "touch" the 9:30 AM opening price. This represents "fair value for the sake of fair value."

### 4. Advanced Inversion Fair Value Gaps
- When price breaks down through an old bullish fair value gap (which initially failed to hold as support), that gap becomes an **Inversion Fair Value Gap** and acts as resistance.
- *Strength Signature:* When price retraces up into a bearish inversion FVG, it should ideally respect the lower half and leave the upper portion completely open (unfilled by candle bodies). A quick wick into the upper portion is permissible, but bodies must remain lower. 
- If price completely rolls over the top of the inversion gap and fills it with bodies, the bearish thesis is invalidated.

### 5. SMT Divergence (Smart Money Tool)
- Analyzing the correlation between the three major U.S. Indices: NASDAQ, S&P 500 (ES), and Dow Jones (YM).
- If one index (e.g., NASDAQ) makes a higher high, but another (e.g., Dow or ES) fails to make a higher high, this constitutes an **SMT Divergence**.
- *The Dow Jones Characteristic:* The Dow is composed of only 30 stocks and is often "sloppy." It frequently acts as the first warning sign (the "crack") that a market move lacks true underlying strength or participation, making it a valuable divergence indicator.

## Important Definitions
- **Inversion Fair Value Gap:** A fair value gap that failed in its original purpose (e.g., support) and is now used for the opposite purpose (e.g., resistance).
- **SMT Divergence:** A divergence between correlated assets (like the major indices) where one breaks a high/low and the other fails to confirm it, signaling underlying weakness or a trap.
- **Opening Range:** The first 30 minutes of regular trading hours (9:30 AM to 10:00 AM NY time).
