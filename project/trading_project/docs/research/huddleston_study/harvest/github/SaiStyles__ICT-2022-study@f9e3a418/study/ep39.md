# Ep39 - The four times of day, and a backtest he tells you to run

**In one line:** He names **the specific times setups form**, states the economics in points per week, and gives an explicit, executable **backtesting instruction** - then says the only thing left to teach is money management.

## The spine

1. **THE FOUR SETUP TIMES, named.** [73:10] Describing how he drilled his son:
   > `initially I forced him into doing that. I said **take a trade at 8:30, take a trade at 9:30, take a trade in the afternoon, and then also sometimes at three o'clock to four o'clock - that market-on-close macro that runs by the algorithm. there's a little setup that forms most days of the week in that last hour.**`

   [73:31] `it might be a buy day, it might be a buy setup, not just a short setup - **but I'm taking you into specific times of the day**`.

2. **THE ECONOMICS - five points.** [72:27]
   > `best case scenario **there's three opportunities for him a day**. he's looking for just **one of them to yield five points**. if he does, **he makes more than half of his weekly salary on that one trade.**`

   The discipline layered on top [72:47]: `once in the morning, once in the afternoon - he's taking **a live trade in one or the other**. but **once he gets one live trade, he's trying to condition himself to do demo** ... he's **not** trying to do three trades a day.`

3. **A BACKTEST SPECIFICATION, stated as homework.** [74:11]
   > `once you see it, and you back test and you pull out example after example of how the market sets up like this and runs to liquidity above or below the marketplace - **count how many times over the last month, three weeks, two weeks, one week: how many setups offered five points with something just like this at these key times of the day. and then do the math.**`

   **This is directly executable and it is his own proposed test of the model.**

4. **SUBMIT TO TIME.** [11:48]
   > `if you are short, you take a partial here, then **you wait for the daily range to fulfill - that means you have to submit yourself to time. what time? three o'clock to four o'clock.** wait to that last hour of trading, because you'll get the delivery of price when it trades down into that.`

5. **He corrects how his own material is being taught.** [06:27]
   > `there's a lot of people all around the world being exposed to my content ... **they're teaching that it's always a relative equal low, or equal lows, to hit those and then go the other way. that's not the case. narrative must be understood** - and narrative unfortunately requires experience.`

6. **The forecast included the day TYPE, not just direction.** [03:05] Going below the equal lows implies a draw all the way into the daily fair value gap, which means [03:24] `a **large range day**` - stated before the open.

7. **He took a loss and deliberately did not chase it.** [03:42]
   > `yesterday I mapped out an idea on Twitter and engaged, and **I took a loss** - a five point loss ... I wanted everyone to see what it's like **even if I do something wrong and I don't rush out there to try to fix it**`

   [79:56] Generalised: `**when you get it wrong you stop. you don't rush to get it back.**`

8. **Money management is the last missing piece.** [79:51]
   > `**I've literally taught you everything except for money management, which we'll talk about next week** - and then you have the complete understanding of what it is you're doing here`

## Contradicts / refines

- **Names the setup times explicitly for the first time.** The clock has been assembled piecemeal across ep02-ep36. [73:10] states it as a list: **8:30, 9:30, afternoon, 3:00-4:00**. Everything else in the corpus is consistent with it; this is the compact statement.
- **Adds a hold rule tied to the clock rather than to structure.** Ep06 [41:26] and ep12 [48:38] tied holding to structure and candles. [11:52] adds: **hold until the delivery window (3:00-4:00)**, because that is when the daily range completes. **A time-based exit condition, which the corpus otherwise lacks.**
- **Explicitly corrects a widespread misreading of his own work** [06:27] - that equal lows always reverse. **Relevant to the corpus: several earlier episodes could be read that way in isolation.** He says narrative decides, not the pattern.
- **Consistent with every stopping rule so far** and adds the per-loss version [79:56]: after a loss, stop for the day; do not chase.

## Reader's note

**[74:11] is the single most actionable instruction in the entire corpus for this project**, because it is a backtest specification stated by the author of the model:

> count, over the last week / two weeks / three weeks / month, **how many setups at these key times of day offered five points** - then do the math.

Every term in it is now defined by the corpus: the setup (ep06/ep24/ep29 gates), the times (ep39 [73:10]), and the yield threshold (5 points). **That is a complete, runnable first experiment** - and it should be the first entry in the hypothesis ledger (DECISIONS section 4), because it is his claim, in his terms, and it either holds or it does not.

Two cautions to carry into it:

1. **The economics assume one trade a day**, not three [72:47]. Three opportunities exist; one is taken live. A backtest taking all qualifying setups tests a different system with different risk.
2. **The five-point target is a *floor* he considers sufficient**, not a target he holds to. Elsewhere he takes 100+ handle moves (ep06, ep11). The five points is the *minimum viable* outcome that makes the week worthwhile - useful as an evaluation threshold, not as an exit rule.

And note the standing promise at [79:51]: **money management is the only thing he says is left.** If it appears in ep40 or ep41, that closes the model.
