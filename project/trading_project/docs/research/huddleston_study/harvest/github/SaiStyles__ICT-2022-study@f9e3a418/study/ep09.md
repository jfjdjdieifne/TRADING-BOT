# Ep09 - "Power Three: Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution - and New York PM Session"

**In one line:** The shape of a day (**open low -> fake move -> real move -> close high**), the **Judas swing** that starts it, and one of the few genuinely mechanical rules in the corpus: **if there was a big overnight run, do not trade the New York morning at all.**

## The spine

1. **POWER THREE - the anatomy of a bullish day.** [02:57] `if I'm bullish I'm expecting the opening price to be near the low of the day or the session, then it trades lower making some important low, and then rallies, creates a high, and then closes near the high of the day`. [03:18] `it's not important for you to try to predict the closing price`.

2. **The JUDAS SWING is the manipulation leg.** [03:31] `Judas swing is the false move, typically in London and in New York session`. [03:38] `there are fake runs that start off a move`. This is the move you hunt: [06:15] `it's going to be most likely a small little move lower - that's the move you want to try to go in and hunt a long`.

3. **The opening price is 8:30, and you draw it forward.** [06:26] `where is the opening price? well, I like 8:30`. [06:31] `you use the opening price here at 8:30, draw that out in time`.

4. **Confluence, stacked and counted out loud.** [06:34] `did we go below it? yes. did we go inside the imbalance? yes. did we take out a short-term low? yes. did we hit an order block? yes. was it an optimal trade entry? yes. lots of factors there.`

5. **THE OVERNIGHT RUN RULE - mechanical, and rare.** [15:15] `if there's a big move overnight for equities - this is not forex, this is just for trading NASDAQ, Dow and e-mini S&P - if there's a big run overnight, **avoid the New York session. don't even mess with the New York session.** wait until the other side of lunch at 1:00 in the afternoon New York time, and then anticipate the New York lunch lows taken out, or the New York morning session lows.`

6. **Do not chase.** [12:08] `when we open up at 8:30, do we go in here and start buying it just because it's gone up overnight? no, we don't chase it`. [12:21] What you wait for: `whenever you see a big run up or a big run down there's a consolidation that takes place shortly after`.

7. **How an order block actually forms.** [07:31] `consecutive down closed candles right before a price surge that has an imbalance - that's how you find your order blocks`. High-probability version [07:43]: bias bullish, **displacement** present, mark the down-close candles, anticipate a return into them.

8. **A wick through the gap does not invalidate it.** [25:34] Answering a comment - `does this invalidate the fair value gap? no it does not`. The test is the **candle bodies**: [25:43] `look at the body of this candle - isn't that respecting it? yes`. [25:55] `I'm permitting a greater level of imperfection in price delivery`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Refines the order block definition from ep03.** Ep03 [24:28] defined it abstractly as `a change in the state of delivery`. Here it is given a **recognisable shape**: the run of consecutive close-candles immediately preceding a displacement that leaves an imbalance [07:31]. Same idea, now findable on a chart.
- **He uses order blocks again, freely.** Fourth data point. Ep05 [36:52] `you don't need an order block`; here they appear in the confluence stack [06:40] and as the bonus setup [27:11]. **The honest reading across ep03/05/07/09: the order block is part of how he actually trades, and the "you don't need it" line means the FVG alone is sufficient for a beginner - not that he avoids them.**
- **Softens ep06's geometry.** Ep06 gave hard gap boundaries. Here [25:34] price trading *through* the gap does not invalidate it, judged on **candle bodies**. A backtest using strict gap boundaries will stop out of trades he holds.
- **Adds the missing "when not to trade" to ep05's day structure.** Ep05 forbade noon-1:00. This adds a **whole-morning veto** conditional on overnight range [15:15].

## Reader's note

**The overnight run rule is the most codeable thing in this episode and possibly the most useful.** It is a genuine filter with a stated threshold condition (`a big run overnight`), a stated action (skip the AM session), and a stated re-entry time (after 1:00 PM). Most of his rules gate *entry*; this one gates *participation*, which is rarer and more valuable.

Two cautions:

1. **"A big run" is undefined**, exactly like displacement. Same problem, same solution needed: pick a threshold, log it in the ledger, and test sensitivity.
2. **[25:34] materially changes the stop logic.** The gap is a *zone of interest*, not a hard boundary - bodies matter, wicks are tolerated. A naive backtest treating a wick through the gap as invalidation will exit trades he stays in, and will underperform the model as taught for reasons that have nothing to do with the model.
