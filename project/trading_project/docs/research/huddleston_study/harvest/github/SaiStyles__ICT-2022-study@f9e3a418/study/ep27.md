# Ep27 - "Counter Trend Ideas"

**In one line:** The same fair-value-gap machinery pointed *against* the higher-timeframe trend - plus an unusually honest account of **execution errors** made while trading from a phone.

## The spine

1. **The counter-trend framing is explicit and optional.** [13:45]
   > `you can frame what I'm teaching **inside of a counter trend model** if that's what you are by nature - a contrarian. **if you want to be trading with the highest probability** and in sync with the early session move, then **trade exactly how I've outlined the principles around that 8:30 to 9:30**.`

   **He ranks them: the 8:30-9:30 session model is the high-probability version; counter-trend is a legitimate but secondary application.**

2. **The draw does not have to be reached to be useful.** [02:03]
   > `the draw on liquidity **does not need to be traded to** to have a profitable framework or setup`

   [02:25] `you look for a setup that will allow you some kind of participation in this expansion towards that - **it doesn't need to get there**`.

3. **Why the counter-trend was licensed.** [08:02] `we're working with the logic that it's probably going to be a **retracement**, because we've been down for a long time now, and most people are probably going to want to get out over the weekend - **so the algorithm is going to start squeezing on them to get their positions squared before the close of the Friday**.`

   Structural support [03:02]: sell-side already swept below relative equal lows, price back **above** them, a second attempt lower **rejected**.

4. **The day's shape - Power Three at session scale.** [05:33]
   - 9:30 runs the **overnight London low** -> manipulation [04:58]
   - Low of day formed; accumulation [05:39]
   - Rally, high at **12:10** (lunch) [06:29]
   - Retracement into **1:30**, `the algorithm will start seeking liquidity` [06:45]
   - Drop through **equilibrium** into the fair value gap at **2:00-2:25** [07:30]
   - **Displacement** -> entry [07:42]

5. **Equilibrium used as a gate, not a target.** [06:58] Asked whether the relative equal lows qualify as a discount: `is that a discount from here to here? **no.**` He marks equilibrium (the 50% Fib) [07:04] and requires price **below it** before buying [06:01]: `I want to buy this discount market ... **because it's at or below equilibrium**`.

6. **Execution errors, reported in full.** [11:40]
   > `I **messed up** on the close of the 20 contracts, because I wanted to take off 20 above here and try to put it in as a limit order, **but it ended up closing as market**`

   [12:02] The cause: `I'm working with TradingView but I'm used to using actual live platforms ... and because I'm doing it **on my phone** and I was out doing other things - real life. **and this is the reason why you don't want to be trading off of your phone.**`

   [13:16] `this was a **failure on my part** - I didn't get the price I actually wanted. none of these closures were actually above the 12,430 level`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Ranks the model's variants for the first time.** [13:45] The **8:30-9:30 session model is the highest-probability form**; counter-trend is a valid but secondary framing. Ep26 took a counter-bias trade without saying where it sat in the hierarchy; ep27 places it. **When the spec is assembled, the 8:30-9:30 version is the primary model and everything else is an extension.**
- **Adds equilibrium as an entry gate.** Ep02/ep16 used the 50% to define premium/discount for *targeting*. [06:58] uses it as a **precondition for entry** - price must be below equilibrium to buy. This is the range-based definition (not ep22's liquidity-based one), used as a filter.
- **Extends ep14 and ep20's honesty about execution.** Ep14 exited early on judgement; ep20 slept through the setup; ep27 fumbled the order type and missed the target price entirely [11:40]. **Three separate episodes where the recorded result diverges from the plan for operational reasons.**
- **Reinforces ep18's phone rule** [12:11] - and here it costs him money, which ep18 only warned about.

## Reader's note

**[13:45] is the most useful line for anyone assembling the model spec**, because it tells you what to build first. Everything the corpus contains can be pointed at counter-trend setups, forex, daily charts and PM sessions - but he states plainly that the **highest-probability configuration is the 8:30-9:30 New York morning model**. That should be the baseline system; the rest are variants to test separately, not to blend in.

The other durable idea is [02:03]: **the draw is a direction, not a requirement.** A setup aimed at a level that price never reaches can still be profitable, because the trade is participation in the *expansion toward* it. That has a direct backtesting consequence - **targets should be evaluated as partial-fill objectives along a path, not as binary hit/miss** - and it reconciles with ep02's `low hanging fruit` instruction to take the nearest objective rather than the furthest.

Note also the pattern now firmly established across ep14, ep20, ep21 and ep27: **his recorded outcomes routinely differ from his stated plan for entirely mundane reasons** - wrong order type, asleep, at the airport, on a phone. That is worth carrying into any evaluation of his results as evidence.
