# Ep19 - The longest episode: nested Power Three, and when to do nothing

**In one line:** Two hours blending his private group's commentary with the YouTube model. Two things in it matter enormously: the **nested Judas swing** (midnight for the day, 8:30 for the session) and an extended, unambiguous lesson on **not trading**.

## The spine

1. **POWER THREE IS NESTED - two Judas swings, two scales.** [99:39]
   > `that move here also is like a **micro Judas swing for the session of New York**. this is the Judas swing on the daily range or the daily candle. so this is building the low of the daily candle, this is building [the low of] the New York session. **both are using the power 3 concept.**`

   [100:00] `this movement below the opening price at 8:30 **and** below the opening price at midnight - **each move lower is the trap**`.

2. **"LAST DITCH FOR POWER 3 IS 8:30."** [96:46] The rule that finally settles the two-opens question:
   > `if the opening price at midnight - if you're bullish - is lower than price is trading at around 8:30 or after 8:30, then it's likely it's not going to be a factor. **then you use 8:30.**`

   And the ideal case [96:58]:
   > `if there is a day like this one where we have the opening price at midnight, we're already below it, and then after 8:30 we're below it again - **still below the midnight candle's opening price - we're really in a discount, we're really really cheap on the day.**`

   [99:22] `does it take indicators to see that? no. did you require some kind of divergence? nope. how about a Fibonacci? nope.`

3. **The whole daily model in one sentence.** [92:22]
   > `looking for the daily delivery on that candle: **Judas swing, buy below the opening price, expect displacement to the upside, run into liquidity, offset, sell to buyers, move to the sidelines - you're done.**`

4. **DO NOTHING DAYS - taught at length.** On GBP [59:36]:
   > `there's nothing here. absolutely zero. nothing in this chart, nothing to trade on, not one thing ... take a deep breath and hear that again - **there's nothing on this chart that's noteworthy.**`

   [61:04] The prescription: `**closing the charts, turning your computer off, and go do something you love to do.**` [61:41] He has not traded British pound - his favourite pair - `for months`.

   [64:22] The core instruction:
   > `**you do not need to trade every single day.** despite what you're thinking and feeling, you don't.`

   [64:56] And the test: `if you can't reasonably outline [where it's going next] - **then you're gambling**`.

5. **The one-contract doctrine.** [65:28]
   > `I promote **one contract**. work on trying to double your account. how long does it take? I don't know ... **work with the lowest leverage and try to double your account. if you don't have the patience to do that, you're a gambler.**`

6. **Do not pick tops and bottoms.** [18:55] `do not try to pick tops and bottoms - you don't need that. **there's a lot of opportunity between intermediate term highs and lows.** I don't mind missing the long-term high and the long-term low.`

7. **The model is fractal across markets and timeframes.** [13:31] The full setup shown on an **hourly** dollar-index chart: `is this a one-minute chart? no ... it's an hourly chart, and that same fractal nature of that pattern I'm teaching you in the YouTube community exists here`.

8. **Frequency claim, corrected.** [98:01] `every week, every day, and it won't stop` - but [98:03] `**it does not mean every single individual market.** it may not happen in the futures - it might be in forex, or it might be in bonds. so you have to be flexible.`

## Contradicts / refines

- **Supersedes ep17's lower-of-two-opens rule with a cleaner one.** Ep17 [18:56] said take the *lower* open when bearish. Ep19 [96:46] gives the reasoning and the general form: **midnight governs unless it is no longer a factor, then 8:30 is the last ditch** - and the strongest signal is price below *both*. Compatible, but ep19 explains *why* and is more general.
- **Adds a second Judas swing the earlier episodes conflated.** Ep09/ep10/ep13 all referred to "the" Judas swing at 8:30 or 9:30. Ep19 says there are **two, nested**: the daily one below midnight and the session one below 8:30 [99:39]. **This resolves the "three different Judas anchors" problem flagged in ep13.**
- **Reinforces and extends the do-nothing rule.** Ep13 [02:05] skipped live trading for a month; ep15 [06:42] one loss then done; ep16 [17:26] max four trades. Ep19 goes furthest: **entire markets left alone for months** [61:41].
- **Qualifies the "every single day" claim** made in ep06 [22:05] and ep07 [41:28]. Here [98:03] it explicitly means *across markets*, not within one.

## Reader's note

**The nested Power Three is the most important structural idea in this episode**, and it dissolves a real confusion in the corpus. Earlier episodes anchor the Judas swing variously at 8:30 (ep10), 9:30 (ep13) and "London/New York" (ep09). Ep19 says the answer is that the pattern occurs **at every scale**: the daily candle has one, the session has one inside it. Both are Power Three. A backtest can therefore look for the same structure at two nested time anchors rather than trying to pick the "right" one.

The `below both opens` condition [96:58] is fully codeable and is his stated best case - `really really cheap on the day`. It is a two-line filter and it is the single cleanest entry-context condition in the corpus.

**And the do-nothing material deserves as much weight as any setup.** Roughly five minutes of this episode [59:36-65:50] is devoted to *not trading*: no setup on the daily, 4-hour or hourly [52:37]; months without touching his favourite pair [61:41]; the explicit test that inability to state where price is going next means you are gambling [64:56]. A backtest structurally cannot represent this - it always takes the trade - which means **any equity curve derived from his rules will overstate activity and understate selectivity** relative to how he actually operates.
