# Ep22 - Narrative, finally defined

**In one line:** After fifteen episodes of using **narrative** as the deciding factor without ever saying what it is, he defines it - and separately gives a **second, different** definition of premium and discount.

## The spine

1. **NARRATIVE, DEFINED.** [12:17] Answering why market structure alone is insufficient:
   > `**market structure is not the answer.** it helps you frame an idea, but the idea must be in alignment with the present narrative. **narrative is: why should the market go where you think it's going to go on that particular day - based on the climate, the economic calendar events, and the volatility that's being offered for that particular trading session or day.**`

   **This is the first actual definition in the corpus.** It has three inputs: *climate* (the broader regime), *economic calendar*, and *available volatility*.

2. **PREMIUM AND DISCOUNT - a second, different definition.** [11:30] Answering a viewer who could not see how price above an old high could be a premium:
   > `**any time the market trades above an old high, that is a short-term premium**, because it's going into liquidity. **any time the market trades below an old low, that's a discount.**`

   And the warning that follows [11:52]: `things can be overbought and still go higher. things can be oversold, or in a discount, and keep going lower`.

   **Note this is not the 50%-of-range definition** used since ep02 [35:47]. Both are his. See Contradicts.

3. **Bodies hold the imbalance; wicks reach for liquidity.** [08:59]
   > `look at the bodies of the candle - see how they're staying inside of that imbalance. **it reaches for liquidity and stops on these wicks, but the bulk of the volume is being held inside this imbalance.**`

   [08:51] The tolerance stated plainly: `this is **permissible price action** - something you have to get used to`.

4. **Stop placement: a stated minimum and a stated preference.** [17:47]
   > `your stop has to be above, at the very minimum, **the candle that creates the fair value gap** ... for good measure, conservative, **up here** [above the swing high]`

   And the objection answered [18:02]: `oh but ICT, that's a big stop - well, look how volatile it is. **do you want to be knocked out of the trade prematurely, or are you just concerned about making the maximum amount of money on your trade, dismissing the level of risk? that's what gamblers do.**`

5. **The entry is the easy part.** [02:20] New students always ask which order block, which entry candle - [02:24] `and that's **the easiest thing** out of all the learning`. What matters instead [02:28]: `once you understand what the market's likely to do, where it's going to draw to, why should it get there`.

6. **1:30 PM, with a reason.** [19:05] `after 1:30 the lunch hour has been smoothed out, and then there's usually some kind of **retracement higher when it's bearish**, or retracement lower when it's bullish`.

7. **He wants to be seen getting it wrong.** [03:46] On the upcoming live streams: `**the best thing that can happen is for me to get it wrong sometimes** - so that way it'll disarm you`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **TWO DEFINITIONS OF PREMIUM/DISCOUNT NOW EXIST, AND THEY CAN DISAGREE.**
  - **Range-based** (ep02 [35:47], ep04, ep10, ep16): above/below the **50% of a dealing range**.
  - **Liquidity-based** (ep22 [11:30]): above an **old high** / below an **old low**.

  These are not the same test and can point opposite ways - price can be above an old high (short-term premium) while still below the 50% of a larger range (discount). **Log as contested.** The reconciliation that fits: the range definition is *positional* within a dealing range; the liquidity definition is *event-based* and short-term. He uses both in this same episode - the range one at [10:23], the liquidity one at [11:03].

- **Defines the term that ep07, ep12, ep13, ep15, ep16 and ep19 all leaned on.** Every one of those used `narrative` as the deciding factor. [12:20] is the definition; **backfill it into all of them.** In particular ep13's order-block formula (gap + candle + *narrative*) becomes far more tractable: the third term is now *climate + calendar + volatility*, which is at least partially codeable.

- **Refines the stop rule.** Ep06 [36:07] said above candle 1 or 2; ep12 [42:45] said above the swing high. Ep22 [17:47] resolves it as a **minimum** (above the gap-creating candle) and a **preference** (above the swing high) - and explicitly argues *for* the wider stop [18:02].

## Reader's note

**[12:20] is the most important single line for anyone assembling the model spec.**

`Narrative` has been doing enormous work across the corpus - it is the third term of ep13's order block formula, the thing SMT confirms in ep07, the reason to hold through a violation in ep15, and the qualifier that makes a swing "intermediate-term" in ep17. All of those were unresolvable while the term was undefined. Now they are not.

The definition also has a practical consequence: **narrative is partly codeable.** "Economic calendar events" and "volatility offered for the session" are both machine-readable. "Climate" is not, and that residue is where the discretion lives - but the term is no longer wholly opaque.

**The premium/discount split is the more dangerous finding.** Two definitions coexist, he uses both in one episode, and a backtester picking one silently will misclassify a large fraction of setups. The range definition is what ep02-16 use for targeting; the liquidity definition is what he uses here to justify entry above the 8:30 open. **They serve different jobs and should be implemented as two separate flags, not one.**
