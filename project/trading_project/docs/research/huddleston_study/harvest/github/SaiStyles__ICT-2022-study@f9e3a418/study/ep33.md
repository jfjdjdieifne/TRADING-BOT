# Ep33 - "If the fair value gap doesn't exist, I don't trust that level"

**In one line:** The clearest separation in the corpus between his method and break-and-retest trading - **the level only tells you where liquidity is; the imbalance is what he actually trades.**

## The spine

1. **NOT SUPPORT AND RESISTANCE - stated with unusual force.** [09:06]
   > `we're using the fair value gap after a run below - think of it like support broken, return back into the fair value gap. **not - listen folks, listen - NOT go back to the old low broken and act as resistance.** that's not how I look at it. **if the fair value gap doesn't exist, I don't trust that level** as an old support broken turned resistance.`

   [09:29] `everybody that comes to me [says] "oh he's just trading break and retest, or he's trading support and resistance" - **I'm not**, because I classify specific levels beforehand`.

   And the division of labour [12:47]:
   > `**the level itself just highlights where the liquidity would be. the imbalance is what I'm keying off of** and what I'm focusing on.`

2. **He refused to trade until a specific level broke.** [10:30] `I wanted this level, the 3915.25 level, to be broken. I wanted that to happen first`. Why [10:48]: on the 15-minute `that gives me that break, whereas all up here it's just consolidated - **I don't want to catch a break below a short-term low and then run relative equal highs**`.

3. **AGGRESSION IS SET BY VOLATILITY.** [11:02]
   > `you could have [used the earlier short-term lows] **if you want to be ultra aggressive**. I wasn't willing to be ultra aggressive **because we have a lot of volatility in these markets right now**, so I want to know that I know.`

   **The rule: higher volatility -> demand a larger structural break before engaging.**

4. **PREMIUM IS RELATIVE TO THE DEALING RANGE IN QUESTION.** [14:26] Answering the objection that price never reached a premium:
   > `if you're looking at it from the relationship of the **short-term dealing range** - this swing high, that swing low - we have our fair value gap ... **equilibrium or above is premium. that's on a one minute chart.**`

   [15:06] `don't be discouraged, or look at it from a perspective that "hey, the market didn't go to a premium" - **you just don't have the experience to see it yet**`.

5. **When premium is not required at all.** [13:36]
   > `if you look at this displacement leg, you're going to want to measure that and say "well, it didn't go to a premium". **I don't expect it to go to a premium in here, because it's going to be in a hurry to get down below that 3855 level for the week.**`

   **A strong higher-timeframe draw overrides the premium requirement.**

6. **Confluence, not a single trigger.** [12:16] `it's a matter of **all these things coming together as a confluence**, not just one thing leading to ... the exercise of the pushing of the button`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Settles what the levels are FOR.** Across ep02-32 he marks old highs, old lows, relative equal highs, previous-day extremes. Ep33 [12:47] says plainly: those levels **locate liquidity**; they are not tradeable in themselves. **The gap is the trade.** This is the cleanest available answer to the recurring "isn't this just support and resistance?" objection, and it has a direct coding consequence: **no gap, no trade, regardless of how good the level looks.**
- **Adds a volatility-scaled aggression rule.** Ep31 [07:41] trailed stops early because of volatility; ep33 [11:02] demands a *larger structural break* for the same reason. **Consistent principle: volatility raises the evidentiary bar.**
- **Qualifies the premium/discount requirement twice.** First, premium is measured against **whichever dealing range is relevant**, including a 1-minute one [14:33]. Second, when a strong higher-timeframe draw is in play, **the retracement may never reach premium and that is expected** [13:44]. **Both qualifications loosen a rule that ep02/ep27 stated absolutely** - log them, because a backtest enforcing premium strictly will reject valid setups.
- **Fourth consecutive episode with an explicit refusal to trade** (ep24, ep30, ep32, ep33). Here the refusal is *temporal* - not "no setup" but "not yet".

## Reader's note

**[09:06] is the line to quote whenever this model is compared to break-and-retest.**

The distinction is real and it is testable: a break-and-retest system enters at the broken level; his system enters at the **imbalance left behind by the break**, and if no imbalance exists he does not enter at all. Those two systems will diverge on a large fraction of trades, and the difference is measurable. **A backtest that enters at levels rather than gaps is testing something he explicitly disavows.**

The two premium qualifications [14:33] and [13:44] are worth flagging as a pair, because together they make premium/discount a **contextual** filter rather than a hard gate:

- measure it against the *relevant* dealing range, which may be very short-term;
- and expect it to be skipped entirely when the higher-timeframe draw is strong and close.

That is honest but it materially weakens premium/discount as a mechanical rule - **it now has two documented escape hatches, both stated by him.** Record it that way rather than implementing it as a hard filter.
