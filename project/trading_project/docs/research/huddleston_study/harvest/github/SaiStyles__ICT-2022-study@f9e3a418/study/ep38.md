# Ep38 - Narrative, again - and why the gap does not need to fill

**In one line:** A second and richer definition of **narrative**, an explicit break with the liquidity-void school (**he does not require imbalances to fully rebalance**), and a lesson on **abandoning a bias** mid-session.

## The spine

1. **NARRATIVE - the second definition, and it is structurally different from ep22's.** [08:07]
   > `**what is narrative? narrative is the understanding of what price should do, WHY, and what things will it encounter to prove that the narrative you are assuming is in place is in fact underway.**`

   Three components: **the expectation**, **the reason**, and - new - **the confirmations that would evidence it**. Ep22 [12:20] gave the *inputs* (climate, calendar, volatility); ep38 gives the *structure*. **Read them together: ep22 says what it is made of, ep38 says what shape it takes.**

2. **HE DOES NOT REQUIRE FULL REBALANCE - an explicit break.** [05:46]
   > `**I'm not trying to insist that these imbalances completely rebalance. that's one of the main differentiations between me and Chris Lorie.**`

   On liquidity voids [05:03]: `he teaches how the market wants to come back down and fill all that area in. **as you probably noticed, I don't teach that** ... there's times when that can occur, but because I understand the algorithm, that is **not likely to occur most times**`.

   What suffices instead [05:24]: `what makes it a fair value gap is the fact that it's **coming down into this area, or inside this area** ... **it's a fair value to buy it** if you're bullish`.

   **A touch or partial entry into the gap is enough. Full fill is not required.**

3. **The draw matters more than the entry.** [06:52]
   > `**I'm not trying to teach you entry patterns** with an importance that getting in at a specific price level is the most important factor. **I'm teaching you how to determine where the market's likely to go to next - where's that draw on liquidity.**`

4. **ABANDONING A BIAS - the episode's stated topic.** [02:48] The questions he sets out to answer:
   > `what did I do, and **how do I go about abandoning a specific bias**? how do I change gears if it is applicable - **sometimes it's not applicable to reverse, it just means move to the sidelines** and say I'm not going to do anything. and **what are the characteristics that would lead to a bias that is changed**?`

5. **The signature that changed his bias.** [03:23]
   > `we're seeing **multiple lows get taken**, and then the market reverses - **while offering really no high probability shorting opportunity.** the 4070 level never got tagged, **liquidity left in place.**`

   Then the upside case forms [04:01]: previous day's high not traded to, plus a fair value gap above - [04:16] `so you have **two premium arrays** that may be a draw on liquidity`.

6. **Higher-timeframe analysis must be carried down, or you are blind.** [11:35]
   > `if you're just staying on one time frame, or at least **not carrying over higher time frame analysis into your lower time frame, you're going to be trading blind** - you have no idea what you're looking for`

   The worked method [11:05]: a gap invisible on the 5-minute becomes visible when you shade the **15-minute** range and drop down into it.

7. **The scale-out drawn before the move.** [12:56] A rectangle marks where half comes off: `once it gets back up in that area again, **I'm going to take half the position off**, and then see if it can run above that high`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Resolves an ambiguity that runs through the whole corpus: how much of the gap must fill?** Ep06 gave exact boundaries; ep09 tolerated wicks through; ep33 required the gap to exist. Ep38 [05:24] settles it: **entering the gap at all is sufficient** - `coming down into this area, or inside this area`. **Full rebalance is explicitly not required**, and he names it as the difference between his method and Chris Lorie's.

  **Coding consequence:** the entry trigger is *touch of the gap's near edge*, not fill to the far edge. A backtest requiring full fill will miss most of his entries.

- **Second definition of narrative, compatible with the first.** Ep22 = inputs; ep38 = structure. Together they make the term operational: **state the expectation, state why, and enumerate the confirmations you would need to see.** That third part is new and is the most testable component - it turns narrative into a checklist rather than a feeling.

- **Adds bias-abandonment criteria the corpus lacked.** Ep18 [12:01] said hold the bias `unless you're proven clearly that you're absolutely wrong` without saying what that looks like. Ep38 [03:46] gives a concrete signature: **repeated liquidity taken + no qualifying setup + the objective never reached**. And crucially [03:00], the correct response is often **the sidelines, not a reversal.**

- **Consistent with ep33 on support/resistance, with one honest concession.** [10:00] `this is one of the instances where resistance broken turns support - **but it digs a little bit past that**`. He immediately reframes [10:14]: `I'm not looking at support and resistance, I'm looking at the fact that we went through this short-term high, **created an imbalance**, and then traded into that`. **The imbalance is still the object; the level is still just its location.**

## Reader's note

**[05:24] is the most consequential clarification in this episode** and it changes implementation directly. Across ep06-ep35 the fair value gap has been drawn with two precise boundaries, which invites a backtest to require price to reach the far edge. He says plainly that he does not require that - and that requiring it is what distinguishes a different school of thought.

**Implement the entry as: price trades into the gap (near edge touched).** Then measure separately how often full fill occurs; that becomes an observation about the market rather than a condition of the model.

The **bias-abandonment signature** [03:46] is the other durable item, and it is unusually well specified for something so discretionary: liquidity repeatedly taken, no qualifying setup produced, objective unmet. All three are checkable during a session, and the prescribed response - stand aside rather than flip - is consistent with every other stopping rule in the corpus.
