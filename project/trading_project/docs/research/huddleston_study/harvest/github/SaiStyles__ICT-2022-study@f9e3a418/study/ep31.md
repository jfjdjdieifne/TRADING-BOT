# Ep31 - "I'm the scout, you're the sniper"

**In one line:** He explains exactly what his public level-calls are *for* - direction, not instruction - and takes a mid-session short that answers a common objection: **what if there's no setup at the open?**

## The spine

1. **THE SCOUT / SNIPER FRAMING.** [03:45]
   > `my style is **we're aiming for liquidity**. so if [price] is up here and I'm pointing down here and taking your attention down there, **I'm acting like a scout. you're the sniper in training** ... I'm trying to point you to where you should be aiming, and **you have to do the work of scoping it up, pulling the trigger**, and dealing with all that.`

   [02:43] What he explicitly does not do: `I don't call out trades ... I'm not going to give you an entry, a stop, a target`. [03:00] `the problem is **you don't have the experience of knowing where it's going to likely go next**` - that is the gap he fills.

2. **A level pointed at is a TARGET, not a floor.** [03:39] Answering the misreading directly: drawing attention to the 16 May low while price is far above it does **not** mean "it should not come back here" - [03:45] `we're aiming for liquidity`.

3. **You do not have to trade the open.** [04:55]
   > `some of you are fixated on **only looking for the setup at the opening**. well, what happens if you don't get a trade at the opening? can't you use a fair value gap? **you have to get involved with the move** if it's going to go down to something like this. yes - that's what I did today.`

   He had been in DollarCAD in the morning [02:04], went to lunch [02:20], and took this trade afterwards.

4. **The trigger: a swing high after the gap is used.** [07:02]
   > `what I saw was this fair value gap here, it hit it, created ... **what's these three candles here? it's a swing high.** so as soon as this candle closed here, I'm gonna go right here at this candle and just get in and go short - **trusting the fact that we've already had a fair value gap, we already went through it, we have a swing high, and it's bearish.**`

   Three conditions, counted: gap used + structure + bearish context.

5. **Stop trailing justified by volatility, not fear.** [07:41]
   > `as soon as we started to break lower and it started moving in my favour aggressively, **I moved my stop - only because the volatility is so high right now.** I just don't want to see it come back and knock me out. **that's not fear, that's just sound money management.**`

   Compare ep06 [41:26], where he warns *against* rushing the stop. The distinguishing condition here is stated: unusually high volatility.

6. **Stops harvested repeatedly on the way down.** [08:26] He narrates the descent as a sequence of liquidity events: stops taken ahead of lunch [08:29], lunch consolidation [08:20], break lower, consolidation, `bumps the stops here` [08:49], sell side [09:04], consolidation, then the low of the day [09:08].

## Contradicts / refines

- **Qualifies ep06's "don't rush the stop" rule with a stated exception.** Ep06 [41:26] warned that trailing early gets you stopped out. Ep31 [07:41] trails early and gives the condition: **exceptional volatility**. Both are his; the discriminator is the regime, not preference.
- **Answers the session-only reading of the model.** Ep27 [13:57] said the 8:30-9:30 window is the *highest-probability* configuration. Ep31 [04:55] makes explicit that it is not the *only* one - a fair value gap later in the day is tradeable if the draw is intact. **Consistent, and worth noting: high-probability ≠ exclusive.**
- **Clarifies what his public calls mean** - relevant to how the whole corpus is read. When he points at a level, it is [03:45] `where we're aiming`, not a prediction of support. Several earlier episodes (ep19, ep20, ep24) contain such calls; read them all as **targets**.
- **Reinforces the ep24/ep30 discipline:** he skipped the entire afternoon [09:18] having taken one trade.

## Reader's note

**The scout/sniper framing is the clearest statement of the corpus's own limits.**

He is explicit that the thing he supplies is *direction* - where liquidity sits and which way price should draw - and the thing he does not supply is the execution decision. That has a direct consequence for the model spec: **the reproducible part of his edge is the draw identification**, and everything downstream (entry refinement, stop, size) he treats as the student's own construction. A backtest can implement his gates faithfully and still not reproduce his results, because the results include judgement he says outright he is not transferring.

Practically useful here: the **swing-high trigger** at [07:07] is stated compactly - gap used, three-candle swing high formed, bearish context - and it is an entry that does not require waiting for a new structure break. It pairs with ep12's aggressive entry (no swing-low break required) as the second documented way he enters *before* confirmation.
