# Ep34 - The Sunday gap opening, and the one time he admits to talking retail

**In one line:** A new, simple, weekly level - **the Sunday session's opening gap, extended across the whole week** - which he says behaves as dynamic support and resistance, and he knows how that sounds.

## The spine

1. **THE SUNDAY GAP OPENING.** [09:44]
   > `the opening gap on Sunday - **if you put it in your charts and just plot it across the entire week**, you'll see that many times there's a lot of valuation around that gap. so it'll be treated as a **dynamic price level**: **if it's above it, look for it to act as support; if it's below it, look for it to act as resistance.**`

2. **He pre-empts the objection himself.** [09:33]
   > `and then it treats it as a **dynamic support resistance level**. did you just hear ICT? **that's blasphemy - he just talked retail. that's a cuss word around here.** well, I'm talking about it in a way where you'll understand it.`

3. **When it works: consolidation weeks.** [10:32]
   > `sometimes the week will just run away from it and won't really come back to it. **but in weeks where there's consolidation - like FOMC** ... those types of events create these choppy range-bound markets that don't give you the real clean take-off one-way directional move. **you have to know how to trade in these environments.**`

4. **How he actually used it - a full round trip.** [06:36] Price goes **above** the Sunday gap opening, trades back down into it, holds, rallies [06:46]; falls short of the relative equal highs [06:50]; retraces **into the gap opening again** - now at the discount low [06:56]; rallies from a fair value gap and a 15-minute order block [07:03] into the target.

   Earlier the same level acted as **resistance** from below [05:24], [06:02].

5. **"SLOPPY" DEFINED.** [07:34]
   > `when I say things are sloppy, it just means that **it's not really giving you a whole lot to work with** - you have very few options and you have to know exactly what you're looking for`

   [07:57] And crucially, a *profitable* move can still be sloppy: `even though it's a nice delivery, **it's not giving you all of the mechanics of entry**`. He calls the morning sloppy and the afternoon `a lot better` [07:29].

6. **"Perfect price delivery."** [03:04] A candle low at **3926.75**; the next candle's high at **3926.75** - [03:14] `that is absolutely perfect, that's perfect price delivery`.

7. **He states his own miss.** [08:30] He called **3953**; the actual high was **3954.50** - `off by one and a half points, or handles`. He had rounded the level up a quarter point from an old high [08:33].

## Contradicts / refines

- **TENSION WITH EP33, AND HE FLAGS IT HIMSELF.** Ep33 [09:06] was emphatic: `**NOT** go back to the old low broken and act as resistance ... if the fair value gap doesn't exist, I don't trust that level`. Ep34 [09:33] then teaches the Sunday gap opening as **dynamic support and resistance** and jokes about the blasphemy.

  **The reconciliation that fits the evidence:** in ep34 he never enters on the level alone - every entry still requires a fair value gap or order block *at* the level [07:03]. So the Sunday gap **locates** the reaction, consistent with ep33's `the level highlights where the liquidity would be`. But he does describe it in support/resistance language, and the two episodes are one day apart. **Log as contested; note the reconciliation but do not treat it as settled.**

- **Adds a weekly-scale level the corpus lacked.** Everything prior is daily (ep18's previous-day extremes, ep25's pre-expansion low) or intraday. The Sunday gap is the first **weekly** reference, and it costs nothing to plot.
- **Defines "sloppy" after using it for 30 episodes.** [07:34] - and importantly decouples it from profitability: a move can deliver and still be untradeable by his mechanics.
- **Consistent with ep32's consolidation-day framing** - both say the choppy environment needs different tools, and both point to specific structural levels rather than momentum.

## Reader's note

**The Sunday gap opening is cheap to test and easy to implement**: one horizontal line per week, plotted from the Sunday session open, carried forward. His claim is falsifiable - *price shows valuation around it, and its role flips with position* - and it needs only intraday data already required for everything else.

**But treat ep33/ep34 as a genuine open question rather than a resolved one.** One episode says levels alone are untrustworthy without a gap; the next teaches a level that behaves as support/resistance. His own practice in ep34 is consistent with ep33 (he still waits for gaps and order blocks *at* the level), but the stated teaching is not. When the model spec is assembled, the safe implementation is the ep33 form: **the Sunday gap identifies where to look, never what to trade.**

Also worth carrying: [07:34]'s definition of *sloppy*. It is the first time a word he uses constantly gets a meaning, and the useful part is the decoupling - **a profitable-looking move that lacks entry mechanics is still a no-trade.** That is the same principle as ep30's sell-off-without-a-setup, stated from the opposite direction.
