# Ep13 - "Advanced Price Action Theory in Action"

**In one line:** Ep12's theory applied live the next day - and it produces the corpus's cleanest formula: **a high-probability order block is exactly three things, and nothing else.**

An extra, unscheduled episode [00:07], recorded because ep12 was `[39:18] a little bit more deeper than you're accustomed to`.

## The spine

1. **THE HIGH-PROBABILITY ORDER BLOCK FORMULA.** [17:45]
   > `that is how you determine your high probability bullish order block: **it must have the imbalance, coupled with the down closed candle, and the underlying narrative that it's likely to go higher to reach for buy side liquidity.** period.`

   And what to discard [18:05]: `there is no engulfing candle, does this - forget all that, you don't need that`. Restated as a formula [18:11]:
   > **`the gap + the down closed candle + the idea it's likely to go for buy side liquidity. that's it.`**

2. **Institutional order flow as a HOLD rule, stated twice.** [13:37]
   > `if the move has been bullish, down closed candles **should not be violated** - they're going to act as support. bearish market moves: up closed candles should not be breached and broken through.`

   [22:56] Applied live: `I'm not worrying about the market as long as it doesn't take out these two down closed candles`.

3. **Why that rule exists psychologically.** [14:12] When bearish and an up-close candle forms, `these are the moments where you get scared ... it eats at you like mental cancer`. [14:37] Even in profit: `the trade's profitable and you're like, I just can't stand it anymore, I gotta get out`. The order-flow rule replaces that feeling with a condition.

4. **The 9:30 open produces a Judas swing.** [18:53] `going into the opening at 9:30 ... this is like a Judas swing`. [19:19] `**don't chase this** - and don't chase going higher right before the equities open at 9:30 either`. The initial move is [18:56] `technically the incorrect move`.

5. **Order blocks nest across timeframes.** [20:24] `that is one order block with a lower time frame order block **and** a fair value gap`. [21:46] `there's a hierarchy - higher time frame to a lower time frame`.

6. **The trade plan must be complete BEFORE entry.** [06:42] You must know in advance: `what will cause them to push the button to get in, where their stop loss is going to be, how much they're going to risk, what they're going to aim for, where their partials are going to be`. [06:53] `**you can't determine them once you enter the marketplace** - that's exactly what I did in 1992`.

7. **He is deliberately NOT trading live.** [02:05] Having hit his monthly target and with `[02:23] Ukraine and all that business`, he refuses the risk: [08:34] losing February's gain `would eat at me all through the month of March`. He runs a demo instead [07:46] to stay `plugged in`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Reduces ep03's order block to a formula.** Ep03 [23:55] gave three conditions (gap, liquidity taken, structure shift). Ep13 [18:11] gives three *different* ones: **gap + close-candle + narrative**. The structure shift is replaced by *narrative/draw*. **These are not the same test** - ep03 requires an event to have happened, ep13 requires a directional thesis. Log both; ep13 is later and stated as the definitive determination.
- **Confirms ep12's order flow rule and makes it operational.** Ep12 [48:38] stated it; here it is used as the live reason not to exit [22:56].
- **Extends the Judas swing to 9:30.** Ep09 placed it in London and New York session [03:33]; ep10 anchored it to the 8:30 open. Here 9:30 - the *equities* open - produces its own [18:53]. **Three different Judas anchors now exist across the corpus.**

## Reader's note

**[18:11] is the most compressed statement of an entry condition anywhere in the corpus** - three terms, no ambiguity, with an explicit list of what to ignore. If any single line should go into a backtest first, it is this one.

But note the tension with ep03 honestly: ep03's order block needed **liquidity taken + structure shift**; ep13's needs **narrative**. A narrative is not a testable event. So the ep13 formula is *simpler to state* but *harder to code* than ep03's, because its third term is a judgement. A backtester will likely need ep03's structure-shift as the operational proxy for ep13's "narrative" - and should record that substitution in the ledger as an assumption, not a fact.

The other thing worth carrying forward: **he stopped trading live because conditions were uncertain and his monthly number was already met** [02:05]. That is a position-management rule that never appears in any "setup" - and it is the kind of thing a backtest structurally cannot capture, because a backtest always takes the trade.
