# Ep11 - Market structure nesting, and which opening price to use

**In one line:** He resolves the midnight-vs-8:30 opening price confusion, then teaches the **three-tier classification of swing highs and lows** he learned from Larry Williams - the thing that turns "lower highs" into a readable hierarchy.

## The spine

1. **TWO opening prices, for two different jobs.** Answering ~half a dozen confused comments [07:26]:

   > [07:44] `if you're looking at the entire daily range you're going to use the opening price at **midnight**. but if you're going to be looking at the **morning session**, you're going to be looking at the **8:30** in the morning opening price.`

   **This resolves the ambiguity running through ep09 and ep10**, both of which used 8:30 without saying why.

2. **Structure is NESTED, in three tiers.** [10:37] Not merely "lower highs":
   - **Short-term high** - a high with a lower high on either side (three candles) [14:01]
   - **Intermediate-term high** - a swing high with *lower swing highs* on either side [10:39]
   - **Long-term high** - the same relationship one level up [14:48]

   His old notation: [14:34] one circle for short-term, `a double halo for an intermediate term high, and then three halos for a long term swing high`.

3. **Why the nesting matters for entry.** [16:07] `I trusted this because we had a swing high here already in effect, and I know this candle is not likely to go above this one, because this one didn't go above that one`. [16:17] **`not because it's lower lows and lower highs - there's much more going on underneath the surface`**.

4. **He is scathing about the flattened version.** [16:30] `it's cringy when you hear people talk like that and they try to reduce it down to ... a support resistance idea, buy the floor and sell the ceiling`.

5. **Enter near the structure high, do not wait for the break.** [18:44] `I'm not waiting for a breakout below that low`. [18:53] `I'm getting up in there where it's an optimal entry`. [18:56] `I don't need to get the highest high - but you need to understand why it should be trading away from the levels you're trying to enter at`.

6. **Wicks are noise, bodies are signal - again.** [09:02] `I know because of the volatility it can color outside the lines, and it's okay`. [09:08] `the bodies of the candles are kind of supporting the idea ... but the wicks I could care less about`.

7. **Not every down-close candle is an order block.** [08:26] Retail `will say look at that, it's a down closed candle, I'm going to buy that ... every down closed candle after a run-up is a bullish order block. **it's not.**`

## Contradicts / refines

- **Resolves an ambiguity, not a contradiction.** Ep09 [06:26] and ep10 [13:33] both anchor on 8:30 without qualification. Ep11 [07:44] says that is correct **for the morning session only**, and the **midnight** open governs the daily range. Any backtest of the ep10 opening range must pick the right one for the timeframe it is testing.
- **Deepens ep05/ep10's three-candle swing.** Those defined a swing. This one **stacks** them into a hierarchy [10:39], which is what makes "which high matters" answerable rather than arbitrary.
- **Reinforces ep09's tolerance.** Ep09 [25:34] said a wick through a gap does not invalidate it. Here the same principle governs entry [09:08] - bodies confirm, wicks are ignored.

## Reader's note

**The nesting is the missing piece for anyone trying to code "swing high".**

Every earlier episode says "a short-term high" or "the swing high" as though it were unambiguous. In practice a 1-minute chart has dozens, and choosing the wrong one changes the entire trade. [10:39] gives the rule that ranks them: a swing high flanked by *lower swing highs* is intermediate-term, and it is intermediate-term highs he positions against [11:33].

For the backtester this is directly implementable - it is a recursive definition, three candles at each tier - and it converts a subjective choice into a computed one. **This is one of the few places where he removes discretion rather than adding it.**

One caution: he entered **before** any break, on structure and the gap alone [18:44]. A backtest that requires the structure break to complete before entry will systematically enter later and worse than he does.
