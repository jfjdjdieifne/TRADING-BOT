# Ep08 - "Applying Institutional Order Flow to Forex Markets"

**In one line:** The same model, unchanged, on EUR/JPY and on a **daily** chart - proving it is not indices-specific or intraday-specific - plus the first target method for when there is no range to work inside.

## The spine

1. **Nothing about the model changes. Only the clock does.** [11:04] `it's simply applying what you've been trained with the futures indices, but applying it to the forex market ... applying it to the time of day relative to New York or London`.

2. **It works on the daily chart.** [01:09] He answers a recurring question - `whether or not this is appropriate to be using for higher time frame charts` - by running the full setup on the EUR/JPY **daily** [01:56]: relative equal lows taken, structure shifted, fair value gap, entry, draw to old highs. **The model is timeframe-agnostic.**

3. **The killzones.** [05:38] `seven o'clock in the morning, 10 o'clock in the morning - that's your New York ICT killzone, that's the time of day that New York session trades form`. London: [10:11] `two o'clock in the morning to five a.m.`

4. **A second structure break can be required - and the yen pairs show why he dislikes them.** [02:05] `here is why I do not like the yen pairs` - [02:15] `they tend to have like a double return to a specific level`. The market takes a swing high, [02:27] `there's no fair value gap there`, drops back, then runs the high **again**. [02:35] `so we have two points of market structure that have moved to the upside`. Only then is there a gap to trade.

5. **Targets when there is no range: standard deviation.** [08:15] With no obvious opposing level, [08:31] `I like to take a fib ... anchor it to a previous swing`, then [08:59] **`standard deviation negative one`** projects the target. He calls the price to the tick: [09:13] `133.153` against an actual high of [09:29] `133.153`.

6. **Currency-strength logic for crosses.** [12:37] `when you look at forex pairs you want to look for that relationship between the instruments that make up your pair`. [12:44] `euro yen - euro is strong, yen is weak ... it's going to make this pair go higher`. Checked on **6E** (euro futures) and the yen separately [12:12].

7. **The horseshoe framing for targets.** [03:16] `just like playing horseshoes - not every single time you throw a horseshoe is it going to go on that post, but you're aiming for it`. [03:56] Whether price actually reaches the old high `is irrelevant`; the draw is what orients the trade.

## Procedure taught

Identical to ep06's specification, with the session clock swapped:

| # | step | at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establish bias from the two currencies separately (strong vs weak) | [12:44] |
| 2 | Mark the killzone: **NY 07:00-10:00** or **London 02:00-05:00**, New York time | [05:38], [10:11] |
| 3 | Price takes out a swing low (for a long) | [06:21] |
| 4 | Price then takes out a swing high -> `[07:18] a valid condition to see if there's a trade` | [07:13] |
| 5 | Work **top-down through the leg**, 5 -> 3 -> 1 minute, hunting the fair value gap | [06:36] |
| 6 | Where two gaps stack, [07:32] expect the possible stab into the lower one but `try to get your entry in on that one there` (the upper) | [07:32] |
| 7 | Target: opposing liquidity if a range exists; otherwise **standard deviation -1** off the prior swing | [08:59] |

**Invalidation:** none stated.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Extends the model's domain in two directions at once.** Every prior episode is intraday index futures. This one proves the same rules run on **forex** and on the **daily** timeframe [01:22]. Nothing in the procedure changes - which is evidence the model is about liquidity structure, not about a market.
- **Adds a target method ep02-07 lacked.** Those all target opposing liquidity inside a known range. [08:15] handles the case where `we don't have a range to trade inside of` - standard deviation -1.
- **Session times differ from the index sessions, and both are his.** Indices: 8:30-11:00 (ep02, ep05). Forex NY killzone: **07:00-10:00** [05:38]. Not a contradiction - different asset class - but a backtester must not apply the index window to forex.

## Reader's note

Two things here matter more than the forex framing:

1. **The model is not intraday-specific.** [01:22] running it on a daily chart, and stating it works on `higher time frame charts`, means the whole ep02-06 machinery is scale-free. That is testable and worth testing - a daily-timeframe backtest is cheaper to run and would independently validate the rules.
2. **OTE is now referenced for the third time and still undefined** (ep01 [24:28], ep07 [27:21], here [09:06] - where he points *outside* this corpus for it). Per DECISIONS section 5, a term used repeatedly but never defined is exactly the dangling reference that decides the reading list. **OTE is currently the top entry on that list.**

The `standard deviation -1` target is genuinely mechanical and precise - he calls 133.153 and gets 133.153 - but the Fibonacci configuration producing it is deferred to a video outside the 2022 playlist. **Codeable in principle, not yet codeable from this corpus.**
