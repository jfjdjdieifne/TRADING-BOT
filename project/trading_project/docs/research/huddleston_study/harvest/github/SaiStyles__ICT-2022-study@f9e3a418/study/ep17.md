# Ep17 - The model on Forex, and the "use the lower open" rule

**In one line:** EUR/USD application, and buried in the last minutes is one of the corpus's few fully mechanical rules: **when bearish, use whichever of the midnight and 8:30 opens is LOWER.**

## The spine

1. **THE LOWER-OF-TWO-OPENS RULE.** [18:41] Answering the recurring confusion directly:
   > `if you're going to be trading FX you're going to be using the 8:30 in the morning for New York session trades, **but refer to the New York open at midnight as well. if the opening price is lower at the 8:30 than that of the opening price at midnight - use the lower one.**`

   The reason [19:04]: `you want to set the **minimum threshold** for a Judas swing or market protraction to the upside when you're bearish`. Inverted when bullish [19:10] - look for the move *below* the 8:30 open to go long.

   **This is fully codeable**: `short_reference = min(midnight_open, 0830_open)`.

2. **INSTITUTIONAL PRICE LEVELS.** [04:53] The round numbers - `the 00 level, the 20 levels, the 50 levels and the 80 levels`. [05:01] `they're very influential because there's lots of liquidity used around those levels - a lot of commerce, a lot of business transactions`. [05:15] `**I dub them institutional price levels**`.

3. **News: the number is irrelevant, the volatility is the point.** [12:34]
   > `what does the ISM PMI number do? **I don't care.** I don't care about the raw data, I don't care what the expectation is.` [12:49] `what I'm expecting is the **volatility** in the marketplace at or around these specific times, because the algorithm is going to use that injection of volatility to facilitate trades.`

   [13:04] And bluntly on whose benefit it serves: `is it trying to give you good trades? no ... **it's a rigged game, you're not supposed to be in it**`.

4. **News is designed to stop you out.** [16:31] `when you have high impact news drivers this is going to many times scare you out of the trade or stop you out ... if you went short back here and started trailing your stop loss, **this is what that's designed to do - take you out**`.

5. **Target selection by convergence.** The Fib standard-deviation levels are chosen where they coincide with the **1.09 big figure** and the daily relative equal lows [06:46]. Result: price reached the extension and the old equal lows, [18:19] `look at the bodies respecting that level`.

6. **Forex killzone differs from indices.** [10:22] `seven o'clock in the morning New York time to 10 o'clock - **this is specific to FX**`. [10:40] `if you're going to trade an index don't worry about that, you can work with the 8:30 to 11 o'clock`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **CONTRADICTS ep12 on the order block, directly.** Ep12 [44:02] was emphatic: `the order block is one consecutive series of up closed candles - **it's not the last candle before the down move** ... stop teaching that`. Here, twice: [11:10] `displacement, fair value gap, **last up close candle** touches it - there's your bearish order block`, and [16:49] `bearish order block, **last up close candle**, the body`.

  **Five days apart, both his.** The reconciliation that fits the evidence: the *series* is the full order block zone, and the *last* candle is the practical touch-point / entry edge within it. But he does not say that - he states each as the definition. **Log as contested; do not pick one silently.**

- **Refines the body-over-wick rule again.** [16:39] `I don't like to use these long tails and wicks ... I prefer to look to price returning back to **the body** of the candle`. Fourth consistent use of bodies-over-wicks (entries, invalidation, projections, now order-block edges).

- **Adds a target-selection criterion the earlier episodes lacked.** Ep12 and ep16 produced Fib levels but chose among them by narrative. Here the tiebreaker is explicit: **coincidence with an institutional price level** [06:46].

- **Confirms ep11's two-opens distinction and then supersedes it with a decision rule.** Ep11 said which open governs which timeframe. Ep17 says what to do when both apply: **take the lower (bearish) / higher (bullish)** [18:56].

## Reader's note

**Two rules here are immediately implementable and neither appears anywhere else in the corpus:**

1. `reference_open = min(midnight_open, 0830_open)` when bearish, `max(...)` when bullish [18:56]. It has a stated rationale - it sets the *minimum* threshold the Judas swing must exceed - so it is not arbitrary, and it removes a real ambiguity that ep09-ep16 all left open.
2. **Institutional price levels (00/20/50/80)** as a target filter [05:15]. Combined with ep16's body-anchored Fib, target selection stops being a judgement call: generate the extensions, keep the ones landing near 00/20/50/80, prefer the one that also sits at prior liquidity.

And one thing to carry as a caution rather than a rule: **he treats scheduled news purely as a volatility timestamp** [12:49], not as information. That is a testable proposition - it implies news *direction* should have no predictive value in the model while news *timing* should matter a great deal. Worth separating in any backtest rather than excluding news days wholesale.
