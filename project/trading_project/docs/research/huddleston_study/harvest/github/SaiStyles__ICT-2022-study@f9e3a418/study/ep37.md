# Ep37 - The breaker, defined - and why he stops trading on Wednesday

**In one line:** A term dangling since ep03 finally gets its definition, and the weekly stopping rule from ep36 gets its full statement and its reasoning.

## The spine

1. **BREAKER, DEFINED.** [00:24]
   > `this is also a **breaker**. okay - **breaker is a low, high, low** - or low, then the high in between, extending out in time. the market trades down into that.`

   **First definition in the corpus.** Ep03 [37:23] named it only to exclude it (`notice there's no breakers`); ep18 [02:31] named it only to say a pattern was *not* one. Here it is finally specified - and it is being used, on the daily, alongside a fair value gap.

2. **STOP TRADING BY WEDNESDAY'S NEW YORK SESSION.** [05:18]
   > `I try to teach my students to **stop trading by the New York session on Wednesday**. so if you had something that was profitable up to that point, **stop trading for the rest of the week** and then observe - just study the price action, but don't try to push any buttons.`

   And the harder version [11:30]:
   > `if you **haven't bagged anything** by that time - from Sunday's weekly open until Wednesday's New York session begins - **don't do any trades**, and deal with that desire of wanting to trade. **that forges discipline, it forges patience.**`

   The reason [05:38]: Thursday and Friday of non-farm-payroll weeks `can be choppy, they can be sporadic, they can come back against you ... **they lose a lot of their precision**`.

3. **He took a bad trade on purpose, to demonstrate.** [10:15]
   > `**I forced myself to engage** - not because I'm breaking rules, not because I'm undisciplined, but **because I want to teach you why I avoid these days: because my precision drops precipitously.** I don't have the visibility on these particular days that I do have on others.`

4. **The built-in advantage argument.** [10:38]
   > `**if you have a built-in advantage, why would you go to an arena where you have built-in disadvantages?** ... you avoid the times where historically your **proven walk-forward real trading results** have been diminished`

   [11:09] `how many times do you have to have your flesh torn open and bleed out before you realise this is probably a time of the month where I shouldn't be doing anything`.

5. **The bias rule, stated simply.** [01:06] `when the market has moved from a low and starts to trade higher and creates a swing low, it's easy to assume it might want to come back up to this high ... **the bias would be expected to be bullish until we take out this high**`.

6. **A conditional bias for the next session.** [03:16]
   > `if it doesn't run out this high tonight - **at 8:30, if we're above the fair value gap high** ... and we have not taken out that high, **I would expect price to try to make an attempt to get to that level.** it doesn't need to go through it.`

   A clean if-then, checkable at a specific time.

7. **Power Three is fractal - and "noise" is a misreading.** [09:18]
   > `if you see this pattern on a daily chart it's easy to understand, but on the lower time frames it's easy to get lost in all the candlesticks ... **that's why the uninitiated will look at it and say it's noise. it's not noise.** it's doing what it does on the daily chart, just being represented on a smaller interval.`

8. **Levels transpose; they are not redrawn.** [06:46] `these levels are **not drawn on the basis of support resistance** on an hourly chart - these are the same levels I drew on the daily, and my changing timeframes from daily to hourly **transposes** them`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **He now uses a breaker, having excluded it from the model.** Ep03 [37:23] listed `no breakers` as evidence the model was stripped down. Ep37 [00:24] identifies one on the daily and uses it as context. **Not a contradiction of substance** - the entry is still the fair value gap - but the exclusion claim from ep03 no longer holds literally. Log it.
- **Completes ep36's Wednesday rule.** Ep36 [08:04] said `done by Wednesday` as personal preference. Ep37 [05:18] makes it a **teaching**, gives the boundary (**Wednesday's NY session**), the measurement window (**from Sunday's open**), and the reason (**precision degrades on NFP-week Thursdays and Fridays**).
- **Provides the corpus's own answer to "is this curve-fitting?"** [10:52] The argument is explicitly empirical: avoid periods where `your **proven walk-forward real trading results** have been diminished`. **He is describing regime selection based on realised performance** - which is exactly what a hypothesis ledger (DECISIONS section 4) is designed to keep honest.
- **Reinforces ep25's "midnight starts the day" with a weekly analogue** - the trading week is measured **from Sunday's open** [11:37], consistent with ep34's Sunday gap level.

## Reader's note

**The breaker definition is worth having, but note what it does not do.** [00:26] gives the shape - low, high, low, with the intervening high projected forward - and he uses it as *context*, not as an entry. The entry in this episode is still the fair value gap. So a breaker joins the order block as a **supporting structure**: it marks where a reaction is likely; the imbalance is still what gets traded (ep33 [12:47]).

**The Wednesday rule is the more consequential item for the model spec.** Combined with everything else, the corpus's stopping rules now form a complete hierarchy:

| scale | rule | source |
|---|---|---|
| per trade | one loss -> done for the day | ep15 |
| per day | max 4 trades; skip AM after overnight run | ep16, ep09 |
| per week | **stop by Wednesday NY session** | ep37 |
| per month | target hit -> stop | ep13, ep15 |
| per regime | avoid NFP/FOMC weeks; avoid choppy markets entirely | ep37, ep19 |

**A backtest that ignores this hierarchy is not testing his system.** It will trade Thursdays and Fridays of NFP weeks - precisely the sessions he says degrade his own precision - and will therefore understate the model's edge while overstating its trade count. That belongs in the spec as a first-class constraint, not a footnote.
