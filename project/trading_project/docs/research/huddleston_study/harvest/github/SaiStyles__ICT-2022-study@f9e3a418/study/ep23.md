# Ep23 - Live FOMC, and the vocabulary he has been using precisely all along

**In one line:** A real-time FOMC session, annotated as it happens - which produces two clarifications the corpus badly needed: **run vs sweep**, and **when to anchor on wicks instead of bodies**.

## The spine

1. **RUN vs SWEEP - a deliberate distinction.** [04:40]
   > `the vocabulary I'm using here is **running** the Tuesday high, **not sweeping** the Tuesday high.`

   [05:29] **Run** = `it runs right over top of it and it just doesn't look back`.
   [05:32] **Sweep** = `it just goes above or below a low or high ... it's going to go up a little bit and then go down and reverse`.

   [05:55] `**I try to pick my words carefully** so that way you understand the distinction between what I'm expecting as a sweep-and-reverse back into the range, or an expansion move.`

   **This is retroactively important.** Every prior episode uses both words; he has been using them precisely and the corpus has been reading them loosely.

2. **WICKS vs BODIES - the rule, finally.** [12:14]
   > `usually I'll use the highest open or close and the lowest open or close in the price swing **when it's not expected to have a whole lot of volatility**. because it's the FOMC, it's more likely to be much more volatile - that means **the extreme of the ranges should be considered. that means now I'm going to use the wicks.**`

   [12:36] `you get asked a lot in the comments - why does ICT use the wicks and why does he use the bodies, and what does he differentiate? **I just taught you.**`

   **Default: bodies. High-volatility events: wicks.**

3. **FOMC has a known shape.** [01:46]
   > `it has like a knee-jerk reaction, kind of like non-farm payroll - there's an **initial leg**, then **another leg**, then like a **fake-out leg**, and then **the real setup comes**`

   Timing [11:34]: `the drop down at FOMC at **2 o'clock**; once it drops down, usually around **2:30** it finishes its initial leg where it prices in whatever it's going to do, and then starts to rally`.

4. **Eight setups in one afternoon - and you should take one.** [10:04] He counts `there's **eight trades** in this whole entire thing`. Then [10:26]:
   > `I don't want you all thinking well, we all have to have the same setup. **no you don't.** ... you may not take any of the other seven out of the eight.`

   [15:00] `I'm not [expecting you to take all these] - any one of these could be a specific setup that fits **your unique model**`.

5. **A gambler's setup, named.** [14:19] The run into FOMC before the announcement: `that's a setup, but **that's a gambler's setup before the FOMC announcement**` - he explicitly does not count it among the eight.

6. **Do not trade FOMC.** [15:51] `I don't want you thinking this is an invitation for you to try to trade FOMC or a non-farm payroll event - **they're very, very risky, extremely risky**`. [16:02] But `they're really fun to go back into and study ... a wonderful case study`.

7. **Proof it is live.** [02:58] `there's no replay button being used here, the candles are painting - the replay button is dim`. He forecasts the level, then narrates price reaching it [08:02].

Ep23 [12:14] gives the **exception**: on high-volatility scheduled events, use the extremes. **Not a contradiction - a conditional rule that ep16 stated unconditionally.** Backfill: ep16's rule is the default, ep23 supplies the switch.
- **Sharpens all prior uses of "run" and "sweep".** [04:40] They are distinct predictions: a **sweep** implies reversal (the ep02-06 liquidity-raid setups), a **run** implies continuation. - **Adds an explicit exclusion to the trade count.** Ep16 [17:26] capped the day at four trades. Here he counts **eight** available setups but stresses you take only the ones matching your own model [15:00]. **Not a contradiction: eight exist, you take one or two.**

## Reader's note

**The run/sweep distinction is the most consequential thing here for anyone reading the rest of the corpus.**

He says he chooses these words carefully [05:55], and he is right - but a reader (or an extraction model) that treats them as synonyms will conflate two opposite predictions. **Sweep = go beyond and reverse. Run = go through and continue.** Anywhere the corpus says "run the highs" it means expansion; "sweep the highs" means the liquidity raid that precedes a reversal. That distinction should be applied when the model spec is assembled, and it is worth re-checking the earlier pages against it.

The **wicks/bodies conditional** [12:14] is similarly a small rule with large effects: it changes where every Fibonacci anchors on high-volatility days, which changes every projected target. Codeable as: *use body extremes by default; use wick extremes when a scheduled high-impact event is in the window.*

And note what he does **not** do: he identifies eight valid setups and takes essentially one, having said in ep16 the daily cap is four. The consistent message across ep16, ep19, ep21 and ep23 is that **the constraint is not finding setups - it is declining most of them.**
