4. **THE RISK LADDER AFTER LOSSES - the core of the money management.** [31:15]
   > `your next trade **you should do half of one percent**` - because after a loss `you're going to be most likely wanting to have **revenge**, and you're probably going to want to do more leverage than you should ... **that is a loser's cycle, gambler's mentality**`

   The recovery condition [31:52]:
   > `by reducing the risk on your next trade once you take a loss, **you have to make back fifty percent of what you lost** ... before you can go back to your full one percent risk`

   And if that one loses too [32:20]: `what size do you use on your next trade? **one quarter of one percent** - and that's obviously the lowest you can go`.

   **The arithmetic that justifies it** [34:22]:
   > `if you take a **1%** loss, then another at **0.5%**, then another at **0.25%** - **you're only down one and three quarters percent**. that's not a lot of drawdown, but you've now taken **three losing consecutive trades. that's easy to come back from.**`

   Versus the alternative [34:47]: 1% -> double to 2% -> 3-4% -> `then you don't use a stop, or you move your stop ... and suddenly **it's very easy to fall into ten percent drawdown**`.

5. **A HARD STOP IS NON-NEGOTIABLE.** [15:13]
   > `you should be respecting the risk by actually **putting a hard stop in**. **if you don't put a hard stop in you're not really respecting the risk** - what you're saying is: I'm risking this trade because I believe I'm right, but if I'm wrong I'll figure out where I'm wrong when it gets there. **you don't want to do that.**`

6. **What to watch instead of the stop.** [14:40] `if you're worrying about that stop loss being tripped, versus following along in price action continuously measuring whether price is giving you what you're expecting - **is the institutional order flow still bullish if you're long, or still bearish when you're short** - those types of things are more important`.

7. **Drawdown reframed as a loan.** [37:16]

## Reader's note - and the close of the corpus

**This episode makes the model fully specified for the first time.** With OTE defined and the sizing ladder given, every component of the system now has a stated form somewhere in the 40 episodes.

**The money-management ladder is the single most important addition** and it is entirely mechanical:

```
risk = 1%
on loss:      risk = risk / 2        (floor 0.25%)
on recovery of 50% of the loss:  risk = 1%
```

Three straight losses cost **1.75%**, not 3%. That is a materially different equity curve from flat 1% sizing, and it is trivially implementable. **It should be tested as a variant against flat sizing** - the comparison is cheap and the result matters more than most entry refinements.
