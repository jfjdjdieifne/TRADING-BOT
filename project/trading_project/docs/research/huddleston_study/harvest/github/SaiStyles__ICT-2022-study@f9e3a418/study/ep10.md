# Ep10 - "Implementing Economic Calendar Events with the Open"

**In one line:** He answers what he calls **the single most-asked question of his career** - how to read daily bias - and hands over the **opening range**: a projected zone below (or above) the open where he says *every* setup of the day will form.

## The spine

1. **The most-asked question, answered.** [03:18] `without a doubt the number one question I get is: ICT can you please teach me the daily bias`. [03:39] `I'm never going to be able to do it any easier than this ... this is really all I teach my students and there is nothing more secret to it`.

2. **Bias reduces to one question.** [18:00] `you just need to know: is this daily range more likely to expand higher or lower than the opening price. that's it. that's all you have to do. that's the key to bias.` [17:52] `take the close out - I don't want you thinking you have to predict the closing price`.

3. **THE OPENING RANGE - the mechanical core of this episode.** [13:30]

   > `the open to the high, whatever that range is - you take that range and you subtract it from the opening price. that is your opening range.`

   And what it is for [13:41]:

   > `that's where your fair value gaps, that's where your stop raids are going to happen, that's where optimal trade entry is going to happen. every potential shorting candidate ... every premium array will reside and form in the range between the open and its high.`

   [14:25] Its purpose: `it gives me an area at which to bracket out how much leeway I can give the market when it starts to go below the opening price and still take a favorable entry`.

4. **Power Three, mapped onto the daily candle.** For a bearish day:
   - **Accumulation** = [13:08] `the open and anything above it - that is accumulation of shorts`
   - **Manipulation** = [16:17] `the initial rally up, that's the sucker play ... it only goes up just to go down`
   - **Distribution** = [16:38] between the low of the day and the close - `what's being distributed? smart money's short positions`

5. **Close proximity entries.** [15:13] When the ideal short above the open is missed, `there's lots of opportunities coming in just below the opening price - this is called close proximity entries`.

6. **A swing is three candles. Not more.** [20:29] `it's three candles that make up a swing low. **you do not need a Williams fractal** - that's way too many candles and you've missed the move`.

7. **Bias persists until the draw is reached.** [22:44] `you're not abandoning the bullish bias until we get above these highs`. He is explicit that losing days inside a correct bias are normal: [22:43] `you might get stopped out, you might have a losing trade`.

8. **News: the move often starts BEFORE the release.** [02:16] `usually there's going to be a buildup or a dropping down in price right before the report comes out, and then the real move starts to ensue`. [02:00] Reports may also be `a smokescreen`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Supersedes ep07's bias method with a better one.** Ep07 [07:35] framed bias as "where is price drawing to". Here it is sharpened to a question about **the daily candle relative to its own open** [18:00], which is both more precise and directly codeable.
- **Gives the Judas swing a measurement.** Ep09 named it [03:33]. Here it becomes the **input to the opening range** [14:08] - the fake move is not just a warning, it is the ruler you measure the day with.
- **Confirms ep05's 3-candle swing and rejects wider definitions explicitly.** [20:31] naming the Williams fractal as wrong is unusually specific for him.
- **Refines ep02's "8:30-11:00" from a session into an anchor.** Here 8:30 is the **opening price** that the whole day's geometry hangs from [13:33], not merely the start of trading hours.

## Reader's note

**The opening range is the most valuable mechanical idea since ep06, and it is easy to miss** because he delivers it in one unbroken sentence at [13:30] with no heading and never repeats it.

Why it matters for a backtester: every prior episode says "find the setup". This says **where the setup will be** - a bounded zone computed from two prices you already know by mid-morning. That converts an open-ended scan into a filtered one, and filters are what make a backtest tractable.

Three notes for coding it:

1. **It is a projection, not a range price traded in.** open - (high - open). Do not confuse it with the opening *range* concept used elsewhere in trading (first N minutes' high/low). His is a mirrored measurement.
2. **It presumes the Judas swing completed.** You cannot compute it until the manipulation high is in, which in practice means waiting - consistent with [12:16] `we don't chase it`.
3. **He claims it is exhaustive** - `every potential shorting candidate` [13:47]. That is a strong, falsifiable claim and worth testing directly: take his qualifying setups from ep02-09 and check how many fall inside the projected zone. If the claim holds, it is a powerful filter; if it does not, it is a bias he is unaware of. **Either result is worth knowing, and this is a cheap test on data you already have.**
