# Ep25 - Which liquidity gets taken first, and rebalancing yesterday's range

**In one line:** Two genuinely new mechanical ideas: **the ORDER in which liquidity is taken can invalidate your bias**, and a large expansion day creates a target - **the low of the day before it**.

## The spine

1. **THE LIQUIDITY-ORDER FILTER - new, and it can kill a bias.** [28:36]
   > `notice that **the buy side liquidity is ran FIRST**. this is really important folks. **if we would have gone down and took the previous low out, then ran up here - that to me is NOT bearish.** this - running above buy side liquidity at 9:30, hitting that **without having this low taken out** - that IS bearish, because it's within the context of the bias.`

   The logic [29:05]: a bearish day should go to a **premium first** (taking buy stops, giving smart money their short fills), *then* seek the discount. Sell-side first breaks that sequence.

2. **REBALANCING THE PREVIOUS DAY'S RANGE - a concrete target.** [31:33] After Monday's large down day, Tuesday retraces up to **Friday's low** - the low of the day *before* the expansion:
   > `it's rebalancing that entire Monday range - it's going back to the previous day prior to Monday, its old low on Friday.`

   And the trap it sets [31:45]:
   > `**it tricks people into thinking that it's made the low** and it's going to keep going up - when the only thing it has done is gone up to a logical level on that daily timeframe that rebalances all of that sell-off.`

3. **A "indecisive" daily candle can contain a clean Power Three.** [33:44] Tuesday's daily candle was a big upper wick, small body - `it looks indecisive`. Dropped to intraday [33:01]: open, rally above the 8:30 open, fair value gap, market structure shift, displacement down. [33:44] `it's inside that daily candle that looks indecisive`. [33:47] `you have to understand what you're looking at **relative to time and price**`.

4. **The 8:30-9:30 interval has a job.** [34:43]
   > `that hour-long interval - we're expecting, when we're bearish, **a run higher to set up shorts**. now the short can form inside that hour, **or it could just provide the leg that sets up the framework**`

   [34:15] 8:30 = news embargo lifts, algorithm starts seeking liquidity. [34:31] 9:30 = the equity open, `that's what this little manipulation is`.

5. **SEASONAL TENDENCY, defined by analogy.** [11:11] Times of year when asset classes `historically have produced price swings that follow a seasonal tendency`. [11:49] The analogy: living in Maryland, `would it be reasonable for me to expect snow on the 4th of July? no.`

   [12:29] The specific one: `about the last week or so of April going into the month of May, these markets - the S&P, the Nasdaq, the Dow, the Russell - tend to be weak`. [12:53] `by itself it doesn't really mean much, but in the hands of someone that's initiated with what I'm teaching, **it is a road map to consistency**`.

6. **The fair value gap is the focus; the order block is not the secret sauce.** [08:40] `that's not the whole of this model, it's not the secret sauce. **the fair value gap is what I'm trying to teach you to focus on** because ... that's the easiest thing for a new trader to sit down and understand right away`.

7. **Do not pick tops and bottoms - his most expensive lesson.** [22:44] `I have lost **more money** trying to do that than any other thing`. [23:44] `there's a lot of meat in between the major turning points`.

## Reader's note

**The liquidity-order filter [28:36] is the most useful new rule since ep19.**

It is a *sequence* condition, and sequence conditions are rare in this corpus - most rules describe a shape. Stated for code: on a bearish day, the first significant liquidity event must be **buy-side taken above a high**. If price instead takes out sell-side below a low first, the bearish premise is not confirmed and the day's setups should be skipped. He gives the mechanism (smart money needs the premium fills before distributing lower), so it is not arbitrary.

**The previous-day rebalance target [31:33] pairs with it.** After a large expansion day, the retracement has a computable destination - the pre-expansion day's extreme - and he explicitly says that reaching it *looks like* a reversal but is not. That is a directly testable claim about where counter-trend retracements terminate, and it needs only daily bars to test.

One caution: the FVG-vs-order-block emphasis has now shifted three times across the corpus (ep03 order-block-centric, ep05 dismissive, ep25 FVG-primary). **The consistent reading across all of them is that the fair value gap is the entry mechanism and the order block is contextual support** - but ep03 remains on record as the most detailed order-block teaching, so the spec should cite ep25 for priority and ep12 for definition.
