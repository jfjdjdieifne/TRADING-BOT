# Ep15 - Vacation-month live entry at the 9:30 open

**In one line:** A live long taken *at the equities open* - the riskiest moment he identifies - and the one episode where he **trades without a stop loss** and explains exactly why, while telling you not to copy it.

Recorded during his stated March vacation [00:15] `I'm on vacation folks`.

## The spine

1. **The 9:30 open is a special case, and stops fail there.** [04:46]
   > `generally I like to trade with a stop loss, but **if I'm trading the open like this, stops can be taken really really easily and then reverse and never give you a chance to be in it**. so I'm allowing myself a little bit of flexibility.`

   Immediately followed by the disclaimer [05:00]: `**I'm not trying to encourage you to trade without a stop loss** - believe me, I'm right here ready to click and close it if it trades below it`.

2. **Manual invalidation replaces the stop.** [04:41] `I'll give it a chance to go below it just by a little bit and I'll close it at market`. The exit is a *judgement executed live*, not an order resting in the book.

3. **Narrative overrides the mechanical gap boundary.** [03:17] Price traded below the short-term low, outside the gap, and he holds:
   > `it does not negate the fair value gap **because I'm using narrative** - narrative is: I expect people to look at that and say okay it's going to go up, and they're already trying to buy right now, but their stop loss is going to be below that swing low`

   Sell stops at **13,612** [03:45] - `they just took those now`. He then calls his own entry [03:47] `a high-risk entry long`.

4. **Target chosen for a round-number equity milestone.** [04:06] `I'm going to aim for the 80 level ... that'll give me a nice figure to run to 50,000 on equity`. Also justified structurally [05:25]: a fair value gap at the 680 level, above equilibrium, i.e. premium.

5. **A one-trade-then-stop rule for the day.** [06:42] `at this moment I could put the stop loss on at my entry, and if it takes me out then I'd move to the sidelines and **wouldn't take any more trades today**`.

6. **News-day caution.** [07:09] `it's important to be aware of the economic calendar because we're probably going to have a lot of volatility later on today`. And on FOMC-type days [06:59]: `you can trade in the morning but you got to be **done early**`.

7. **He stops trading for the month once the number is hit.** [08:43] `rounding out the day and the month of March - **I will not be taking any more trades this month**`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Directly qualifies ep09's gap-tolerance rule.** Ep09 [25:34] said a wick through the gap does not invalidate it, judged on bodies. Here he goes further - price took out the swing low *entirely* and he stayed, justified by **narrative** [03:21], not by candle bodies. **This is the loosest invalidation standard anywhere in the corpus.**
- **Contradicts his own stop discipline.** Ep06 [36:07], ep09 [22:05] and ep12 [42:45] all specify a resting stop. Here there is none [04:46]. He flags the inconsistency himself and restricts it to the 9:30 open.
- **Adds a session-level risk rule the setup episodes lack.** [06:42] one loss -> done for the day; [08:46] monthly target hit -> done for the month. Together with ep13 [02:05] (uncertain conditions -> don't trade live), this is now a consistent pattern: **he stops trading far more readily than any "setup" episode implies.**

## Reader's note

**This episode is the clearest evidence that his live results are not the mechanical model.**

Three deviations in nine minutes: no stop loss [04:46], holding through a structural violation on narrative [03:21], and a target chosen partly to hit a round equity number [04:13]. Each is defensible in context and he is transparent about all three - but a backtest of the *taught rules* would not have taken this trade the way he took it.

For the model spec this matters in a specific way: **"narrative" is now load-bearing and still undefined.** It has been the deciding factor in ep07 (SMT only confirms narrative), ep12 (the two questions), ep13 (the third term of the order block formula), and here it overrides a technical stop-out. It is the single most-used undefined term in the corpus after OTE, and unlike OTE he never points to an outside video for it.

Practical note for a backtester: **the 9:30 open needs separate treatment.** Ep09 said skip the morning after an overnight run; ep13 said don't chase the 9:30 move; here he says stops don't survive there. Three episodes converge on the same conclusion - **the equities open is a distinct regime and probably should be excluded or modelled apart** rather than pooled with 8:30-11:00.
