# Ep02 - "Elements To A Trade Setup": the whole model, in one lesson

**In one line:** He gives away the entire framework here - top-down bias, liquidity run, structure break, fair value gap entry, premium/discount target - and every later episode is an expansion of something stated in these 49 minutes.

**Read this page before any other.** If you only ever read one episode, it is this one.

## The spine

1. **Start at the weekly candle, and only ask one question.** [13:04] Before the week begins, `[13:11] get a read on what you think that next weekly candle is going to do is it going to go higher or is it going to go lower`. [13:16] Explicitly **not** predicting the close. That single call `[16:31] sets your initial bias for the week`.

2. **The daily chart is where the draw lives.** [16:35] `on the daily chart you're looking for swing highs and swing lows to get your liquidity`, and [16:42] `the draw on liquidity what makes the market go higher or lower it's predominantly found on this time frame`. [16:51] `majority of your analysis should really be linked to this time frame`.

3. **Price draws to exactly two things.** [21:30] `it's drawing to one of two things ... it's drawing to stops which is liquidity or it's running to an imbalance`. [17:46] `above old highs buy stops, below old lows sell stops`.

4. **Before a big move, expect the opposite move first.** [22:35] `anytime a significant price move lower is expected always anticipate some measure of a stop hunt on buy stops or a short-term high being taken out`. [22:51] Reversed for longs. He insists it is [23:03] `occurring almost on a daily basis`.

5. **Why the hunt happens - the counterparty argument.** [21:57] Driving above the high `[22:01] sends all those buy stops into market orders flooding the marketplace`, which [22:04] `gives a huge influx of willing buyers at a high price which is the perfect counterparty to smart money that wants to sell at a high price`. **This is the mechanism the whole model rests on**, and he states it as mechanics, not sentiment: [30:56] `you're led to believe it's buying and selling pressure ... no it's not it's liquidity`.

6. **Then drop timeframes and wait for structure to break.** [27:55] After the liquidity run, `the market creates a short-term low here ... and then it breaks below that this is key this is called a break in market structure`.

7. **The entry is the fair value gap left by that break.** [29:12] Once the low is broken `you're going to look for this little area here that's that imbalance`, and [29:21] `the market's going to go right up inside that area there and that's where you want to sell`.

8. **Target using premium/discount, and take the near one.** [35:22] The liquidity matrix is just the range split at 50% by Fibonacci - [35:50] above is `premium ... it means it's expensive`, below is `discount`. Aim at [48:02] `an old low or an imbalance`, and [48:09] **`you want to get the closest target ... low hanging fruit is how I teach`**.

## Procedure taught - THE SETUP

He restates it cleanly himself at [43:10] and again as homework at [47:25]. Bearish version; invert for bullish.

| # | trigger | condition | at |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Weekly bias set | Expect the weekly candle to expand LOWER | [13:04] |
| 2 | Daily context | Locate old highs/lows - where liquidity rests and price is drawing to | [16:35] |
| 3 | **Run on liquidity** | Price runs ABOVE an old high / relative equal highs, taking buy stops | [43:13] |
| 4 | **Break in market structure** | On a lower timeframe, price breaks BELOW a short-term swing low | [43:21] |
| 5 | **Imbalance forms** | That break leaves a fair value gap - candle 1 high to candle 3 low, unoverlapped | [43:42] |
| 6 | **Entry** | Price trades back UP into the gap -> sell short | [43:47] |
| 7 | Stop | Above the run-high, or above the entry candle's high - `whichever your risk parameters allow` | [32:36] |
| 8 | Target | Split the range at 50%; aim at the nearest old low or imbalance | [47:57] |

**Invalidation:** none stated explicitly. The stop placement at [32:36] is the only exit-on-wrong he gives. He does say at [33:56] that chasing after the low is taken out is the mistake: `once we take out a low ... it becomes a matter of your chasing price`.

**On confirmation - he explicitly rejects it.** [34:18] `you want to learn to trust going short when the market's going higher and that feels scary at first`. [34:40] Waiting for confirmation is `someone that's coming in late that doesn't have a read on price`.

## Reader's note

Three things here decide whether a backtest of this model works or fails:

1. **Step 3 and step 4 must be in that order.** A structure break *without* a preceding liquidity run is not the setup. He is explicit at [10:10] that the break is `significant only on the basis that we have taken liquidity out of the marketplace`. Code the sequence, not the pattern.
2. **"Nearest target", not the furthest.** [48:09] is a rule, not a preference - and it is the kind of thing that quietly halves a backtest's win rate if you code the daily low as the target instead.
3. **He rejects confirmation entries outright** [34:40]. A backtester that waits for a close back below the gap is testing a different, later, worse entry than the one he teaches.
