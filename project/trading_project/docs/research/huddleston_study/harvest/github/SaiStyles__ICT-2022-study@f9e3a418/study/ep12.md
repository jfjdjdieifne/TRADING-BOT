# Ep12 - "Market Structure for Precision Technicians"

**In one line:** The deepest episode in the corpus. He states his **one original contribution** - *a rebalanced imbalance creates an intermediate-term high or low* - and from it derives a **stated invalidation rule**, a **failure signature**, and the definition of **institutional order flow**.

[00:38] `this is actually going to be a lesson even my paid mentorship group has yet to see`.

## The spine

1. **The only two questions he asks before trading.** [06:48]
   > `is it likely to go up for buy side liquidity or buy stops, or to rebalance an imbalance? or is it likely to go lower to sweep short-term lows for sell side liquidity, or down to rebalance an old imbalance?`

   [07:00] `that is the number one question I have before I sit down in front of my charts`. Everything else - patterns, harmonics, Elliott, ratios - [07:11] `I don't care`.

2. **THE ORIGINAL IDEA: a rebalance creates an intermediate-term swing.** [13:24]
   > `every single time price rebalances an imbalance ... that swing high that's created at that moment, I immediately label in my mind as an **intermediate term high**` - and a rebalance from above creates an **intermediate term low** [14:11].

   [22:42] He is explicit this is his, not Williams': `what he's not teaching in that book, what he hasn't taught in any of his works`. [23:21] `no one taught this to me - I kind of pioneered this idea`.

3. **So an intermediate-term high has TWO definitions.** [41:27]
   - a short-term high with **lower short-term highs on both sides**, OR
   - **the main one:** [41:41] `it trades back up to rebalance an imbalance - that immediately becomes an intermediate term high`

4. **THE FAILURE SIGNATURE.** [37:52] An intermediate-term high *should* exceed the short-term highs flanking it. When it does not:
   > `if the intermediate term high is not higher than two short-term highs, that is telling you the market is very weak and the algorithm is tipping its hand`

5. **THE INVALIDATION RULE - stated, explicit, rare.** [41:54]
   > `if we're bearish and it rebalances like this, the next short-term high should be lower than that intermediate term high. **if it trades higher than that, then your trade idea is probably flawed. don't force that trade. wait for market structure to get back in sync.**`

   [50:47] Restated: `that intermediate term high or low should not be violated - that's a key high or a key low`. [51:23] `if they are violated, that means your idea is wrong and you cancel the idea`.

6. **INSTITUTIONAL ORDER FLOW, defined.** [48:38]
   > `when you're bearish, all of your up closed candles should keep price from going higher than them`

   [51:49] They are `speed bumps` - price may touch them and react, `but you do not want to see them traded above if you're bearish`. Inverted when bullish: down-close candles should support [51:04]. **This is the hold-your-trade rule.**

7. **THE ORDER BLOCK, corrected in the strongest terms yet.** [44:02]
   > `the order block is **one consecutive series of up closed candles** - it's not the last candle before the down move`

   [24:46] `everyone else is teaching the idea of the last up close candle before the down move. **that is not my order block. so you need to stop teaching that** - call it something else, but don't call it order block.`

8. **The higher timeframe is PARENT.** [10:57] `since I am expecting the daily chart to be the parent of this price structure, all minor lower time frame swings are going to be subordinate to it`. A long-term high broken = [11:33] `then I'm probably wrong in my analysis`.

### Two entries

**Aggressive** [39:32] - inside the order block, on a fair value gap, **without waiting for a structure break**:
> [37:12] `if I get a fair value gap form with a breakdown like this - notice **I don't need a swing low broken**`

Justified by structure alone: the intermediate-term high says the market is weak, so the next high must fail [39:44]. Stop above that high [42:45].

**Classic** [42:49] - `low risk, high confirmation`: price trades below the order block, returns to it, enters on the gap. Stop above that candle's high [43:03].

## Contradicts / refines

- **Settles the order block question that has run since ep03.** Ep03 defined it abstractly, ep05 said `you don't need` it, ep07/09 used one anyway. Here he gives the definitive form - **a consecutive series** [44:02] - and attacks the single-candle version directly. **Read this as the authoritative statement; ep05's dismissal was about beginner sufficiency, not about validity.**
- **Adds the invalidation the corpus was missing.** Ep02-11 give stops but almost no *idea-invalidation*. [41:54] gives one: a short-term high exceeding the intermediate-term high cancels the thesis. This is the single most useful addition for a backtester after ep06's geometry.
- **Refines ep11's nesting with a mechanism.** Ep11 classified swings by shape. Ep12 says the **rebalance event** creates the classification [13:24] - a cause, not just a pattern.
- **Contradicts the ep02/ep06 requirement for a structure break.** [37:12] `I don't need a swing low broken` for the aggressive entry. Ep02's step 4 and ep06's step 3 both require it. **Both are his: the aggressive entry trades structure without confirmation, the classic waits for it.** Two models, not one.

## Reader's note

**This episode contains the two things a backtester most needs and the corpus had been missing.**

1. **A thesis-invalidation rule** [41:54]. Every prior episode gives a *stop* - a price where the trade dies. This gives a *condition where the idea dies*, which is different and more useful: it tells you when to stop looking for the setup at all rather than re-entering into a market that has changed.
2. **A hold rule** [48:38]. Institutional order flow answers "when do I stay in?" - while up-close candles keep capping price, the bearish move is intact. Ep06 gave a trailing rule tied to structure; this gives a continuous one tied to candles.

Two cautions:

- **The aggressive entry has no structure-break requirement**, which makes it a genuinely different system from ep02/ep06. Do not merge them into one backtest. Test them separately - they will have different win rates and different failure modes, and pooling them will hide both.
- **His Fibonacci settings are shown on screen and never spoken** [33:08]. The -1.5 standard deviation projection is therefore **not reproducible from the transcript**. This is a concrete instance where the video channel holds something text cannot - and it is exactly the kind of thing DECISIONS section 19's deferred video experiment should target.
