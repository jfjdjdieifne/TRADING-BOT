# Ep05 - "Intraday Order Flow and Understanding the Daily Range"

**In one line:** He introduces **displacement** - the filter that separates a real setup from a failed one - and then reduces the entire model to **two patterns**, explicitly discarding order blocks and breakers.

## The spine

1. **DISPLACEMENT is the missing filter.** [18:15] Answering a criticism that one of his examples would have lost: [18:44] `if you have a children's swimming pool ... and you have an elephant just fall down inside of it, the water's going to be displaced`. Applied: after price runs an old high and turns down, [18:49] `you want to see an obvious displacement - you don't want to see it just, like, a little lethargic run here, that's not enough`. **This is why some structure breaks fail: the break was anemic.**

2. **He walks a whole morning showing displacement failing, repeatedly.** [20:46] onward is a sequence of near-misses: an `[20:53] energetic break below that? no, it's just a very weak anemic move lower`; then a wick that snaps back - `[21:06] does that create a fair value gap in that? no, it's not there yet`; then finally [21:21] `it trades up and then smashes down, then the next candle closes - what do we have? there's your fair value gap. that's your short.` **Four rejections before one acceptance.**

3. **THE TWO PATTERNS - the whole model, reduced.** [36:36]

   > `wait for a swing low to be violated and then rally. what if you don't get a swing low that trades below it? you look for a move higher that's sudden - displacement higher - then look for a fair value gap. if it trades back down in the fair value gap you buy that.`
   > [36:48] `there's your two patterns. that's it. that's the only two patterns you need. you don't need 15 different gimmicky names. you don't need breakers, you don't need an order block.`

4. **He is actively de-emphasising order blocks.** [07:21] `yes I'll look for order blocks, but I'm going to try to stay away from order blocks in this lesson, in this mentorship really, because I have models that don't even rely on order blocks`. [07:30] `I'm teaching fair value gap here and that is the main focus because it repeats, it's easy pattern`. **Read ep03's order block lesson through this.**

5. **The day has three parts, and one of them is forbidden.** Morning move (8:30-11:00), New York lunch (**no trade**), afternoon move (1:30-4:00). [10:04] `noon to 1:00 in the afternoon New York time - that is a no trade time period ... not even in demo`.

6. **1:30 PM starts the afternoon, for a stated reason.** [32:35] `I'm preferably looking for a swing high and swing low to form at 1:30. why 1:30? because there's an algorithm macro that starts running at 1:30 - that's beyond the scope of this mentorship, but just trust me`.

7. **The afternoon is the morning's logic, repeated.** [33:00] `that same basis of looking for a stop hunt in the morning ... I'm looking for the same thing here. that's it, same thing.`

## Procedure taught

### Pattern A - stop hunt then reversal (bullish shown)

| # | step | at |
|---|---|---|

| 3 | `[33:58] that small little stop hunt is all that's necessary` - starts a **buy program** | [34:04] |

### Pattern B - displacement then fair value gap

| # | step | at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No swing violation available | [36:32] |
| 2 | **Sudden displacement** - energetic, `like that elephant`, not lethargic | [36:39] |
| 3 | A **fair value gap** forms inside the displacement | [36:42] |
| 4 | Price retraces into the gap -> enter | [36:45] |

**Invalidation:** none stated for either pattern, but [19:45] he acknowledges losses plainly: `sometimes the market will have some kind of news event ... or it just simply rolls over top of it. that's a losing trade. that's why you have to have a stop loss`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Refines ep03 hard.** Ep03 [23:55] made the order block the entry, requiring three conditions. Here he says [07:21] he will `stay away from order blocks` and [36:52] `you don't need an order block`. **These are not compatible as equal emphasis.** The reconciliation he gives: the order block is real but optional; the **fair value gap is the primary entry** because it repeats more.
- **Adds the missing filter to ep02/ep04.** Ep02's step 4 was "break in market structure"; ep04 gated on "is there a gap". Neither said the break must be *violent*. Displacement [18:44] is that gate, and it retroactively explains ep04's anemic rejections.
- **Extends the no-trade rule.** Ep03 [34:01] said no trades after noon. Here it is sharpened: noon-1:00 is forbidden [10:04], but **1:30-4:00 is explicitly tradeable** [32:24] - which ep03 had called out-of-scope [34:33]. He changed his mind and says so: [11:30] `I wasn't going to teach that, but because I see a lot of nonsense on YouTube, I'm going to`.

## Reader's note

**[36:48] is the most important sentence in the corpus so far.** He reduces everything to two patterns and names what to discard. If a backtester models anything beyond these two entries, it is modelling something he explicitly said was unnecessary.

Two cautions for coding:

1. **Displacement has no numeric threshold.** He describes it entirely by analogy and contrast (`energetic` vs `anemic`, `animated` vs `lethargic`). This is the single largest discretionary hole in an otherwise mechanical model - and it is load-bearing, because it is the difference between ep04's four rejections and its one entry. **Any backtest must invent a threshold he never gave, and that choice will dominate the results.** Flag it in the hypothesis ledger.
2. **The order-block conflict with ep03 is real and unresolved as of this episode.** Do not silently pick one. Both statements are his, three days apart.
