# Ep29 - SMT gets its name, and the 50%-of-displacement filter

**In one line:** The first time "SMT" is spoken in the corpus - and a **new, specific, codeable filter** that disqualifies a fair value gap based on where it sits inside the displacement leg.

## The spine

1. **THE DISPLACEMENT-LEG 50% FILTER - new, and the most codeable thing here.** [05:11] Explaining why an apparently valid short setup did **not** qualify:
   > `yes, you can see there is a rally up, it breaks the short-term low, a fair value gap, it trades up into it and breaks down - big deal. **this doesn't fit the criteria, because the displacement leg here would require us to see a fair value gap ABOVE 50 of the high and the low of the displacement leg - and we don't have that.**`

   [05:24] `**nothing here fits the model for a short.**`

   **Stated for code:** measure the displacement leg (its high to its low). For a short, the fair value gap must sit **above the 50%** of that leg. Below the midpoint, the setup is rejected.

2. **SMT, NAMED.** [04:24]
   > `we had a divergence here and here. **no indicators folks** - I'm using intermarket relationships, and this is what I dub **SMT: smart money technique, or smart money tool**`

   The observation: NASDAQ made a lower low while the S&P `said no, once again I'm not going lower than the low I made at 10 o'clock` [04:18]. **First naming in the corpus** - the concept appeared unnamed in ep07 as `cracks in correlation`, 22 episodes earlier.

3. **Its role is still confirmatory.** [04:36] `the importance of that is **I already had a bias that's bullish**`. [04:42] `there was no interest in wanting to be short - I want to focus on being long`. Consistent with ep07's warning that SMT confirms rather than generates.

4. **CLOSE PROXIMITY ENTRIES, explained properly.** [07:07]
   > `you might not get so precise with your entries, you might fumble with your order entry, or something might happen like it happened with me ... **as long as you're getting in there really close** - or like I'm suggesting here, a **close proximity entry** - I don't think there's anything wrong with it`

   [11:00] And demonstrated: `this was **not** entering in the fair value gap, it was **in close proximity to it**`.

5. **Eight candles of opportunity.** [08:13] After the structure shift and return to the gap: `look how many opportunities - one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight - **eight candles** it gave an opportunity to get in this area, either inside the fair value gap or in close proximity to it`.

6. **Pre-Fed days are low-volatility by design.** [03:15]
   > `we're in a day before Fed chair speaks, so there's going to be **very low volatility** as a result of that - so it's going to be a **small range day**`

   [03:42] `it's important to know when the volatility is likely to occur, **and when the volatility is likely to shrink up**`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Adds a fair-value-gap location filter that no earlier episode states.** Ep06 [20:29] required the gap to be **inside the displacement range**. Ep29 [05:11] tightens it: the gap must be in the correct **half** of that range - **above 50% for a short**, below for a long. **This is strictly stronger than ep06 and it rejects setups ep06 would accept.** Log it as a refinement and test it separately - it is exactly the kind of filter that changes a backtest's trade count materially.
- **Confirms the builder's SMT ruling with the source's own naming.** The concept is now traceable end to end: unnamed in ep07 [24:54], named here [04:29], reused in ep35 and ep38. Its role never changes - confirmation of an existing bias, never a trigger.
- **Formalises "close proximity entries"** which ep10 [15:13] named in passing. Here it is demonstrated with real fills outside the gap and the heat measured [11:00].
- **Adds a volatility-regime rule.** Ep17 said news creates volatility; ep29 [03:15] says the **day before** a Fed speaker suppresses it. Together these bracket the calendar: know when volatility arrives *and* when it will not.

## Reader's note

**[05:11] is the find in this episode and it is easy to miss** because he states it while rejecting a trade rather than while teaching a rule.

The filter: after displacement, measure that leg's high-to-low. For a short, the fair value gap must sit in the **upper half**. A gap in the lower half means price has already delivered most of the move and the entry is too late - which is consistent with everything he says elsewhere about not chasing, but this is the only place it is given as a **number**.

It composes cleanly with the other gap filters now established:
1. gap must be **inside** the displacement range (ep06)
2. gap must be in the **correct half** of it (ep29)
3. next candle must **open away** from the gap (ep20)
4. displacement must **close beyond** the swept level (ep06/ep24)

**Those four are all structural, all codeable, and none requires an invented magnitude threshold.** Together they are the closest thing the corpus has to a complete mechanical definition of a valid setup - and they should be implemented as separate gates so a backtest can report which one does the rejecting.
