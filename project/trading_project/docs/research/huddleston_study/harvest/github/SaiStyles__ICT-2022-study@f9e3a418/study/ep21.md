# Ep21 - Risk on / risk off, and what it means when the Judas swing never comes

**In one line:** The dollar index drives both forex *and* indices - and when price refuses to rally above either opening price on a bearish day, **that refusal is itself the signal**.

## The spine

1. **RISK ON / RISK OFF - why DXY matters for everything.** [02:10]
   > `dollar if it is going up - **this is a risk off scenario**. risk off implies that generally every other market or asset class will start to decline, and this is treated as a **flight to quality** or a **safe haven**. money pours into the dollar and it pours out of risk assets.`

   [02:52] `then also this applies as well to **the stock market and index market**`. So DXY is the barometer for both FX and equities.

2. **THE NO-JUDAS SIGNAL - the most valuable idea in the episode.** [17:06]
   > `did we rally above the opening price at midnight? **no.** did we rally above the opening price at 8:30? **no.** and we're bearish. what does that indicate to you? ... **it's extremely bearish. it can't even rally to a short-term premium above the opening price at key levels and time.**`

   And the consequence [17:34]:
   > `we have an extremely heavy bearish market, so it's not going to give us these stunning little short-term rallies that we can fade. it's just going to stay heavy - and **either you get in where you can find small little pockets of imbalance, or you miss the move entirely**.`

   [19:27] `when it's extremely bearish **you have to trade it differently**`.

3. **The two opens, mapped to two Power Threes - confirmed.** [13:21] Midnight `is useful for trading the **London session** ... it helps me frame the power 3 for the **daily range**`. [16:07] `the opening price at 8:30 I use for power 3 for the **New York session**`. Consistent with ep19.

4. **He was wrong, and says so plainly.** [10:00]
   > `listen closely folks, because this is one of those times where **I didn't get it right and I have no problem telling you.** I was expecting this to rally a little bit higher and maybe flirt with that 4320 level ... **that did not happen.**`

   [13:53] `in this instance **I missed a setup**`. [14:01] `I'm human`.

5. **ORDER BLOCK LEVELS - four of them, including a new term.** [04:56] He references `the **low** of the daily order block, the **open** of the order block, and the **mean threshold** - halfway - and then the **high** of the order block being **the less likely level to be traded to**`.

   **`Mean threshold` = the 50% of the order block.** New terminology, and the "high is least likely" ordering is a usable ranking.

6. **Daylight savings, dismissed.** [11:26] `all of that is resolved if you simply just listen to the instructions ... toggle it to New York`. [11:55] `set a clock on your computer that always tracks the local time in New York, 24/7, 365`.

7. **Real life costs money, and he says so.** [17:56] His wife needed a lift to the airport; he captured **$2,000** and missed the afternoon entirely. [18:28] `if I would have just held on to what I had in the morning, I could have done around **twelve thousand dollars** today alone. but I didn't hold on to it - I had other things to take care of. **that's the reality of things.**`

## Contradicts / refines

- **Confirms ep19's nested Power Three and assigns the sessions explicitly.** Midnight -> London + daily range; 8:30 -> New York session [13:21], [16:07]. Ep19 established the nesting; ep21 names which session each anchors.
- **Adds a *negative* reading of the same structure.** Every prior episode uses the Judas swing as the setup. Ep21 uses its **absence** as information about trend strength [17:24]. **This is new and it inverts the logic** - the same observation now produces a conclusion whether it happens or not.
- **Adds order-block internal levels.** Ep03/ep12/ep13 treated the order block as an opening price or a candle series. Ep21 gives it **four levels** - low, open, mean threshold, high - with the high least likely to be reached [05:02].
- **Reinforces the honest-error thread.** Ep13 (skipped the month), ep15 (no stop), ep18 (demonstration trades), ep20 (slept through it), ep21 (wrong about the rally, missed $10k to a school run). **Cumulatively this is a consistent and unusually candid record of the gap between the model and its operator.**

## Reader's note

**The no-Judas signal is directly codeable and I have not seen it stated anywhere else in the corpus.**

The rule: on a bearish day, if price never trades above the midnight open *and* never trades above the 8:30 open, the market is in a strong trend that will not offer the standard fade entry. Two consequences follow, both testable:

1. **Do not wait for the retracement entry** - it is not coming [17:41].
2. **Expect the day's range to be one-directional**, so the "small pockets of imbalance" become the only entries [17:48].

That is a genuine regime classifier built from two comparisons you can make by 8:31. It converts a *missed* setup into information, which is rare in this corpus - almost everything else tells you what to wait for.

The **mean threshold** [05:02] is worth flagging for later episodes: it is introduced casually here as the 50% of an order block, and the four-level ranking (low / open / mean threshold / high, high least likely) is a usable entry-refinement scheme that no earlier episode provides. Watch whether it recurs - if it does, it belongs in the spec; if it appears once, it is an aside.
