# Ep30 - Sweep the lunch lows, then run

**In one line:** The PM continuation rule stated as a compact recipe - **if bullish and price consolidates through lunch, find the lunch lows, wait for them to be swept, then the move resumes** - plus a clear case of a sell-off that produced *no setup*.

## The spine

1. **THE PM CONTINUATION RECIPE.** [11:57] Stated as a summary at the end:
   > `if you're bullish and it consolidates in lunch - **find the lunch lows, wait for it to run that out, and then they'll resume**`

   Earlier, live [01:26]: `I taught you in the mentorship that many times **when there's an afternoon continuation to the upside, you'll see that the lows at lunch time will get swept** - and we're seeing that here`.

2. **A SELL-OFF WITH NO SETUP - stated explicitly.** [08:03]
   > `notice that **my model did not give a setup** this afternoon - it didn't give the setup in here. even though it didn't run buy side, **it did sell off, it did not give a setup. notice that. it's important to notice that.**`

   And the point [08:12]: `some of you are fearful - what happens if I take a trade and it's wrong? ... **there are times when the market does sell off, but it doesn't give us a setup**, and then it creates the real move the other direction where you can find my setups`.

3. **The manipulation, narrated in terms of who gets hurt.** [02:21] The failed run at the target: `long holders were **raked across the coals**, not permitted to profit on the 12,553.25 run`. [02:37] Buy stops triggered on breakout buyers, then [02:50] `they get squeezed out ... they just bail out below the relative equal lows`. [03:02] Morning longs who trailed stops below the lunch lows get taken too - [03:06] `**they don't want them being able to profit on the real move**`.

4. **3:00 PM spool.** [05:11] `at **three o'clock** the algorithms will start to spool price in the direction for the liquidity - which was that 12,553.25 level, which it hammers`.

5. **The near-miss that mattered.** [00:33] The pre-Fed Judas high came in **one tick** short of the morning high: 12,535.50 vs 12,535.75 [00:40], [00:53]. `it fell one quarter of a point, or one tick, short of that high`.

6. **Bottom-up analysis, unusually.** [00:13] `I'm gonna kind of work backward today ... start with the one minute chart and work our way back out to a higher time frame - **a bottom-up analysis**`. Every other episode goes top-down.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Turns ep05/ep22's lunch observations into an actionable rule.** Ep05 [10:04] said do not trade noon-1:00. Ep22 [19:05] said expect a retracement after 1:30. Ep30 [11:57] says what to *do* with it: **the lunch lows are the liquidity that fuels the afternoon continuation.** That is a specific, locatable target rather than a time to wait out.
- **Reinforces ep24's non-setup argument with a different shape.** Ep24: the setup never formed, so no trade. Ep30: a **real sell-off occurred** and still produced no qualifying setup [08:03]. **Together they establish that "price moved" and "the model fired" are independent** - which is exactly what a filter is supposed to achieve.
- **Adds 3:00 PM to the intraday clock.** Ep05 had 3:40/3:50/4:00 for market-on-close; ep22 had 1:30 for the PM re-entry. **3:00 PM as the spool toward the day's liquidity target** [05:11] sits between them and is new.
- **Consistent with ep21's honesty about missed fills** - [05:48] he wanted the imbalance entry and was too slow.

## Reader's note

**The lunch-low sweep is the cleanest PM rule in the corpus** and it is fully codeable: mark the high and low of noon-1:00 NY; on a bullish day, wait for the **low** to be taken; treat that sweep as the trigger for the afternoon continuation toward the day's draw. Inverted for bearish.

It also explains *why* the noon-1:00 no-trade window exists - **the lunch range is being built as liquidity**, not as opportunity. That reframes a rule that has read as arbitrary since ep05.

**And [08:03] deserves weight equal to any setup.** He watched a genuine sell-off produce nothing his model would take, and flagged it deliberately. Combined with ep24, the corpus now contains **two explicit demonstrations that the filters reject real moves** - which is the strongest available evidence that they are filters rather than descriptions. Any backtest should be able to reproduce both rejections; if it takes trades on those days, the implementation is wrong.
