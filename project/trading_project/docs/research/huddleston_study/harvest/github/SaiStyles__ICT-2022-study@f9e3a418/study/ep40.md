# Ep40 - Weekly expansion over daily bias, and the 10-11 exit

**In one line:** He wraps the teaching with two of the most useful rules in the corpus: **take 80% off between 10:00 and 11:00**, and **stop trying to call a daily bias - call the weekly expansion instead.**

## The spine

1. **THE 10:00-11:00 EXIT - London close profit-taking hour.** [17:29]
   > `that time frame 10 to 11 - **that's London close**. so if you're looking for directional plays and you're in a trade, **you want to have about 80 percent of your trade off between 10 o'clock and 11 o'clock in the morning** - because usually, not all the time but usually, **that's when it creates the opposing end of the daily range**. so if it's creating the high of the session here, the low is likely to form between 10 and 11. **that's algorithmic.**`

   [18:28] `I've always taught that 10 o'clock until 11 o'clock in the morning is **London close profit taking hour**`.

   **Exception** [18:04]: on crude-oil-inventory days when trading CAD, widen the window to **10:00-noon**.

2. **DAILY BIAS - the simplification, and it is a refusal.** [20:44]
   > `**every day bias is unrealistic.** if I go in looking for a specific predetermined daily bias before the market starts trading, **invariably I'm going to have it incorrect.**`

   [21:24] What he does instead:
   > `**I'm not trying to get a daily bias every single day. I'm trying to determine the likely WEEKLY EXPANSION.**`

   The weekly questions [21:34]: `I utilise a **weekly chart** ... does it look like it's going to run to an old low? does it look like it's going to run to an old high? or is it running to an imbalance below the market price, or above? **or is it likely not to move because there's no data for it that week?**`

   [22:18] `I'm looking for **the direction of an expansion move**. I'm not trying to get the entire weekly range - I'm not trying to buy the weekly low and sell the weekly high.`

3. **MARKET SELECTION VIA THE ECONOMIC CALENDAR.** [46:26]
   > `a lot of you ask all the time - **how do you know which pair to trade, how do you know which one to pick? I'm starting that with the economic calendar.**`

   And the corollary [21:58]: markets **without** a high- or medium-impact driver `will probably be a **lackluster market environment**`, because the algorithm is working the ones that have them.

4. **NEWS TIER FILTER.** [08:14] `**yellow events I'm not interested in. red or medium impact** - those are the news drivers I'm looking for.`

5. **SMT FOR PAIR SELECTION - a use not seen before.** [46:34]
   > `if euro dollar had a high impact news event, I would compare pound dollar and euro dollar for correlation - **so I would look for SMT divergence to signal which one I'd rather be in.** just because the news driver is in the euro, **the better trade may be in pound dollar** ... if they both went down prior to the news, and **pound dollar failed to make a lower low, I'm buying pound dollar**.`

   [45:45] The term for it: `**the relative strength leader** - that would be the better buy`.

6. **Wait for the news, then take the setup.** [09:43] You *could* enter before the release, but `the economic drivers at 8:30 **could have sent it up higher** ... it's a matter of **waiting for the news to hit the market and then create the setup** - that's a little bit more conservative`.

7. **Take partials because you cannot know.** [12:52] `that's the reason why you want to take partials - **because you don't know if it's going to come all the way back up** and revisit the fair value gap, and [you] lose the opportunity to bank something`.

8. **Swing high, restated with the Williams rejection.** [04:54] Three candles - `**I'm not using a Williams fractal** ... five candles - if you're waiting for five candles, **you missed the boat**`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Substantially revises the daily-bias teaching of ep07, ep10 and ep18.** Those built a *daily* bias from the daily chart. Ep40 [20:44] says attempting that **every day** is unrealistic and that he does not do it - he forecasts the **weekly expansion direction** and lets the day resolve within it. **This is the most significant late revision in the corpus.** It does not invalidate ep10's opening-range mechanics, but it changes what those mechanics are applied to.
- **Adds a time-based exit that overrides target-based exits.** Ep02 said take the nearest objective; ep39 said submit to time until 3:00-4:00. Ep40 [17:36] says **most of the position should be off by 11:00**. Read with ep39, the shape is: **80% off by 11:00, remainder held for the 3:00-4:00 delivery.**
- **Turns SMT into a selection tool, not just a confirmation.** Ep07/ep29/ep35 used it to confirm a bias. [46:34] uses it to **choose which instrument to trade** among correlated pairs. New function, same mechanic.
- **Makes the economic calendar the first step, not a caution.** Ep10 and ep17 treated it as a volatility warning. [46:31] makes it the **market-selection filter**: trade where the driver is.

## Reader's note

**Two rules here materially change any backtest.**

1. **80% off between 10:00 and 11:00** [17:36]. This is a hard, time-based, size-based exit rule with a stated mechanism (London close creates the opposing end of the day's range). It is trivially codeable and it will dominate the equity curve far more than most entry refinements. **Nothing earlier in the corpus states it.**

2. **Forecast the week, not the day** [21:29]. This reframes the whole model's top layer. The practical version: use the weekly chart to pick a direction and a draw, then trade the sessions inside it - and accept that individual days will run counter. It also supplies a *skip* condition: **a week with no high/medium impact data for that market is expected to be lackluster** [21:58], so trade elsewhere.

Together with the market-selection rule [46:31], the pre-session routine now has a clear order: **calendar -> pick the market -> weekly chart -> expansion direction and draw -> then the intraday model.** That is the first time the corpus states the sequence from the top.
