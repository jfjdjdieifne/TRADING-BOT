# Ep04 - Two worked examples, and the first explicit NO-TRADE

**In one line:** Eight minutes of him walking the ep02/ep03 setup across two real days - and the most valuable part is the **setups he rejects**, because that is where the rules become falsifiable.

## The spine

1. **No fair value gap = no trade. Stated twice, both as rejections.**
   - [01:44] Price breaks below the old high, `but does it create a fair value gap in here? no` - so he waits.
   - [06:40] Relative equal highs are taken, `does it break down and give you a fair value gap? no it doesn't - so it wouldn't even meet the criteria there`.
   [06:45] He pre-empts the objection directly: `some of you might be looking [and] say well how do you know it doesn't get you on a trade here - listen to the rules I gave you, they're very specific`.

2. **Wait; do not force.** [07:03] `you have to wait for the setup, you can't force it, you can't think it's there - it's there or it's not`. [06:55] The framing is `forcing the market to show you its hand`.

3. **8:30 AM is the hunt.** [03:05] `8:30 in the morning that starts the hunt - basically that's what 8:30 in the morning is to me`. Not a session open in his framing; **it is the time the stop run happens.**

4. **Start on the 5-minute and work down.** [06:13] `this is the time frame you start with and you work down`. First concrete statement of where the top-down cascade begins for intraday.

5. **The range is low-to-high of the day, and equilibrium is the 50%.** [01:17] Drop the Fib from the day's low to the day's high, [01:22] `get your equilibrium price point`, then target below it.

## Procedure taught

Same setup as ep02/ep03, executed twice. The order, restated by his own walkthrough:

| # | step | at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mark the day's low and high; drop a Fib; find equilibrium (50%) | [01:17] |
| 2 | Price runs into a **premium** - `it's really expensive` | [01:33] |
| 3 | **Run above an old high / relative equal highs** - buy-side liquidity taken | [07:09] |
| 4 | **Break below the short-term swing low** -> bearish market structure shift | [03:17] |
| 5 | **Check for a fair value gap.** No gap -> NO TRADE, keep waiting | [01:44], [06:40] |
| 6 | Price rallies back into the gap -> **short there** | [03:35] |
| 7 | Target an old low or imbalance **below equilibrium** | [02:31] |

**Invalidation:** none stated. He gives no stop on either example - only entries and targets.

**Worked numbers (example 1, ES 26 Jan 2022):** short ~4419, cover ~4382 [04:06]. He deliberately shaves the extremes - `[04:13] just to be, you know, not perfect`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Refines ep02 and ep03 with a hard filter:** the fair value gap is not merely the *entry location*, it is a **necessary condition**. Ep02 [43:42] implied it; here it is used twice to reject an otherwise-complete setup [01:44], [06:40]. **A backtester must treat "no FVG" as no-trade, not as "enter at the structure break".**
- **Adds to ep03's timeframe cascade:** ep03 said 3/2/1-minute for execution [28:03]. Here the *start* is named: **5-minute** [06:13].
- **Sharpens ep04's view of 8:30:** ep02 called 8:30-11:00 the window [42:40]; ep03 called 8:30 the start of hours [33:51]. Here 8:30 is given a *function* - it is when the stop hunt fires [03:05].

## Reader's note

This is the highest-value-per-minute episode so far, for one reason: **it contains negative examples.**

Ep02 and ep03 show the setup working. Ep04 shows two situations that look like the setup, fail one condition, and are correctly passed on. A rule you have only ever seen succeed is not yet a rule - it is a description. The two rejections at [01:44] and [06:40] are what make the FVG condition testable.

For the backtester: **the sequence has a gate at step 5.** Liquidity run + structure shift with no resulting gap is a *rejected* setup, and coding it as an entry will generate trades he would never take.
