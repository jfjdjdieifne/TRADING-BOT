# Ep35 - Mean threshold, and why some reversals never happen

**In one line:** The **mean threshold** finally gets its full definition and its signal value - and a new filter, the **high resistance liquidity run**, explains when a counter-trend setup should simply be rejected.

## The spine

1. **MEAN THRESHOLD - fully defined.** [00:38]
   > `when we're looking at a candle that we're using for like an order block: **the low, the open, and the middle of the range** - those are the three sensitive areas that you're watching for. if the market trades up into the middle point of that candle, **this is what I call the mean threshold.**`

   Its signal value [00:58]:
   > `**mean threshold was taken today, and that to me bodes well for a continuation** to take out this short-term high`

   How to find it [01:08]: `take your fib and run it across this candle to the 50 level`.

   **This completes what ep21 [05:02] introduced in one line.** An order block now has three tradeable references, and taking the middle one signals continuation.

2. **HIGH RESISTANCE LIQUIDITY RUN - a rejection filter.** [12:02] Asked what would constitute a short here:
   > `we have all this range in here that it's not likely to go up to go straight all the way through all this stuff - **that's a high resistance liquidity run.** not that it can't happen, but **it's likely to not form or pan out, because we had so many supporting ideas in here - down closed candles are supporting price.**`

   [12:29] The conclusion:
   > `it's likely to go up **just to keep going higher, not go up to go down**. because to go up to go down, we have all of these ranges that we would have to pierce and break - and that requires a lot of ... **intent. not selling pressure.**`

   **Stated for code: count the supportive structures a reversal would have to break. Many = high resistance = reject the reversal.**

3. **SMT - how to plot it, and when it lies.** [06:24] On TradingView: add the other index as a **compare** chart, then change the plot from close to **low** - [07:04] `because you're comparing lows`.

   When it appears [07:27]: `at certain times - **what times? 8:30, 9:30, at news events being released**`.

   **The warning** [07:36]:
   > `if you have a bias it's helpful. **but if you don't know where it's going, you're going to many times encounter what would look like SMT divergence and then it disappears** as they start moving in concert with one another. so you have to be careful with that.`

   [07:53] `that's why I always teach: **know what it's reaching for**`.

4. **The discount ladder - the repeating cycle.** [10:19] He walks the whole advance as one repeated unit:
   > dealing range low -> dealing range high -> retrace into **discount** -> fair value gap -> rally -> new dealing range -> repeat

   [11:33] `the market keeps going higher, **picking up discount levels each time** - the algorithm is picking up more accumulated longs to press deeper into all of this range`.

5. **Compression before expansion.** [08:44] Price sitting under the daily order block: `it's being compressed, basically like a spring, or ... **more like a bomb** ... waiting - and then all of a sudden it explodes`.

6. **He admits a rushed, imperfect entry.** [08:01] `I had very little time to work with anything this morning - I just hurried up, saw the chart, **I missed the actual ideal entry**, I just used a **close proximity entry** with an order block`.

## Contradicts / refines

- **Completes ep21's mean threshold.** Ep21 [05:02] listed it among four order-block levels with no definition. Ep35 [00:56] defines it as the 50% of the order block candle **and gives it a signal**: taken = continuation likely. **Now codeable.**
- **Sharpens ep07's and ep29's SMT warnings into a mechanism.** Ep07 said divergence alone blew his accounts; ep29 said it confirms an existing bias. Ep35 [07:36] explains *why it fails*: **the divergence can simply disappear** as the instruments re-converge. That is a concrete failure mode and it explains the earlier warnings rather than repeating them.
- **Adds a reversal-rejection filter no earlier episode provides.** The corpus is rich in entry filters; [12:02] is the first that says **when not to expect a turn at all** - because the structural cost of reversing is too high. It pairs naturally with ep12's institutional order flow (opposing-close candles as speed bumps): the more of them stacked, the higher the resistance.
- **Reinforces ep33's premium/discount flexibility** with the ladder [10:19] - each new dealing range redefines discount, so "discount" is continuously re-measured as the move develops.

## Reader's note

**Two immediately implementable additions.**

1. **Mean threshold** [00:56]: `(order_block_high + order_block_low) / 2`. Trading into it signals continuation toward the next objective. Combined with ep21's ranking (the far edge of the order block is `the most unlikely level to be traded to`), the order block now has a full internal geometry: near edge, open, mean threshold, far edge - with decreasing likelihood.

2. **High resistance liquidity run** [12:02]: before accepting a counter-trend setup, count the supportive structures between price and the proposed target. Many supportive down-close candles (bullish case) means the reversal must break all of them, and he expects it to fail. **This is the structural counterpart to ep26's counter-bias licence** - ep26 said *when you may* fade the trend (repeated failure to follow through); ep35 says *when you should not* (too much supporting structure in the way).

Also worth carrying: the SMT plotting instruction [07:01] - **compare on lows, not closes**. Anyone reproducing his SMT observations from close-based charts will see different divergences than he does.
