## Contradicts / refines

- **Completes ep12's targeting method.** Ep12 [32:53] gave `-1.5 standard deviation` anchored intermediate-term-high to long-term-low, settings unspoken. Ep16 supplies **the anchoring rule in words** - highest/lowest **body** [09:07]. Together these are now *nearly* reproducible; only the numeric Fib levels remain visual-only.
- **Confirms ep11's two opening prices** [05:32] and adds that the candle interval is irrelevant - only the 8:30 timestamp matters.
- **Consistent with ep09/ep11's body-over-wick principle**, now extended from *invalidation* to *measurement*. This is the third distinct use of the same idea: bodies confirm entries (ep11), bodies define invalidation (ep09), bodies anchor projections (ep16). **Treat "bodies, not wicks" as a corpus-wide rule.**

## Reader's note

**The body-anchoring rule is the single most codeable thing in this episode and it fixes a real problem.**

Anyone implementing his Fibonacci projections from ep12 alone would anchor to the extremes - the wick high and wick low - and get different numbers. He says explicitly that this is why Fibonacci "doesn't work" for most people [13:55]. Changing the anchor from extreme to body is a one-line change in code and it moves every projected level.

Two cautions:

1. **The 4501.25 hit is one example.** He presents it as proof of precision; it is one observation. The method is worth testing precisely *because* the claim is so strong - and testing it is cheap, since it needs only daily bars and the anchoring rule.
2. **He states the level only becomes meaningful when it coincides with an independent draw** (order block, equal lows) [12:53]. So the projection is a **filter on candidate targets**, not a target generator. Coding it as the latter would be using it in a way he explicitly warns against - and would be vulnerable to exactly the "thousand lines on your chart" criticism he pre-empts at [11:36].
