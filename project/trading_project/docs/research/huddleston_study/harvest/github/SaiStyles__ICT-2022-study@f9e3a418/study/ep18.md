# Ep18 - The chart layout, and previous day's highs and lows

**In one line:** His actual screen setup and step-by-step routine - plus two admissions that change how his live account should be read, and a target framework (**previous day's high and low**) that no earlier episode states.

## The spine

1. **PREVIOUS DAY'S HIGHS AND LOWS ARE THE INTRADAY TARGETS.** [18:56]
   > `we're going to use previous day's highs and lows like institutional mindset traders do. **there's lots of liquidity around daily highs and lows. and high-frequency trading algorithms attack previous day's highs and lows.**`

   And the practice, from his earliest public teaching [19:14]: `highlight the previous day's highs and lows - **and do that for the last 3 days**`. [19:20] `you'll see a plethora of setups ... **you're never going to run out of trades, ever**`.

2. **The daily draw is NOT the intraday target.** [18:04] The daily chart's draw-on-liquidity is context. `but is that your trade target? **no. not for intraday trading.**` [18:12] `so you frame your daily highs and lows - previous daily high, previous daily low`. **This distinction is new and it matters:** ep02-16 repeatedly aimed at the daily draw; here he separates the *directional context* from the *achievable target*.

3. **Bias is a FILTER, not a forecast.** [12:01]
   > `a bias is just an idea that you want to work within, and employ your short sale when you're bearish ... **and you're filtering out the other side of the marketplace.** unless you're proven clearly that you're absolutely wrong, you just simply wait for the setups to form with that bias in mind.`

   [11:55] `your bias isn't going to be perfect. and here's a newsflash - I'm not always perfect with my bias either`.

4. **Risk-to-reward is not required for profitability.** [13:32]
   > `I was thumbing my nose at this idea that a risk to reward model is essential for you to be net profitable. **that's not true.** I've proven that with a live account.`

   Asked whether a low win rate can still double an account [13:26]: **yes** [13:29].

5. **Some of his live executions are DEMONSTRATIONS, not setups.** [10:40]
   > `a lot of those executions you're seeing is just simply me pushing a button to show that yes, you can do those types of things to overcome initial fear. **it's going to cost you commission. it's going to look like you have no idea what you're doing.**`

   [10:14] He is answering paid students' questions by *manufacturing* the situations they ask about, live.

6. **What he would discard if starting over.** [06:22] `I wouldn't worry about doing anything with central bank dealers range. I wouldn't worry about doing anything with Asian range ... **unless I've mentioned it in this model, I wouldn't even do any investment of time at all**`.

7. **Not a breaker.** [02:26] Correcting a common misread of high-low-higher-high: `many of you would see that as my bearish breaker. **mhm - it's not.**`

## Contradicts / refines

- **Adds a target layer the corpus was missing.** Ep02 [48:02] said nearest old low/imbalance; ep06 [45:44] said external range liquidity; ep16 [11:30] said the Fib/order-block convergence. **None of them said "previous day's high and low"** - which is simpler, always available, and is what he actually frames the day with here [18:12].
- **Softens the R:R framing throughout the corpus.** Ep09 [22:48] worked a ~3.5:1 example. Here he says R:R is not required at all [13:34]. Not a contradiction of the arithmetic, but a correction of emphasis: **his edge claim is directional accuracy plus liquidity targets, not a reward ratio.**
- **Materially qualifies every live-account result shown since ep02.** [10:40] Some executions exist to demonstrate psychological points for paying students, not because a setup was present. **Any attempt to evaluate the model from his account statements must account for this**, and he says the statements `[10:55] isn't going to mean anything to you unless you understand what I'm really illustrating`.

## Reader's note

**Two things here are more useful than anything about the setup itself.**

1. **Previous day's high and low, for the last three days** [19:16]. It is the cheapest possible framing tool - two lines per day, no interpretation - and he says it guarantees a supply of setups. For a backtester it is trivially codeable and gives an objective target set that does not depend on identifying an order block or drawing a Fib.

2. **The demonstration-trade admission** [10:40] is the most important disclosure in the corpus so far for anyone treating his results as evidence. Combined with ep15 (no stop at the open), ep14 (discretionary early exit) and ep13 (stopped trading live for the month), the picture is consistent: **his account reflects teaching decisions as much as trading decisions.** That is not a criticism - he states it plainly - but it means the account curve cannot be read as a backtest of the model.

Finally, [12:01] is the cleanest statement of what bias is *for*: it does not predict, it **excludes half the setups**. A backtest should implement bias as a directional filter on entries, not as a signal - and should expect the bias itself to be wrong a meaningful fraction of the time, by his own admission [11:57].
