# Definition

Across every series, ICT insists that trading outcomes depend more on psychology and routine than on setup knowledge: managing the fear of being wrong, structuring the day around killzones ([/concepts/killzones-and-time.md]), and treating trading as a professional schedule rather than a lottery.

# Mechanics

- **Time management**: trade only during defined windows; the rest of the day is for review, family, and rest — overtrading is framed as the primary account killer.
- **Fear of being wrong**: addressed by fixed-risk sizing and one-shot-one-kill selectivity so no single trade carries emotional weight ([/concepts/risk-management.md]).
- **Demo vs live**: ICT prescribes proving a model on demo/sim before risking capital, then scaling size gradually as consistency is documented.
- **First-year expectations**: expect little or negative income in year one; treat it as tuition. Longevity and survival are the real edge.

# Application

The [If I Could Go Back](/sources/if-i-could-go-back.md) four-part retrospective is the core statement of these themes; the [New Forex Traders Guide](/sources/new-forex-traders-guide.md) operationalizes them into a study plan, while [Real Money Real Results](/sources/real-money-real-results.md) models live execution discipline.

# Evolution

Psychology content shifted from generic motivation in early mentorships toward process engineering — journaling by session, algorithmic timing logs, and strict routines in 2022–2025 material.
